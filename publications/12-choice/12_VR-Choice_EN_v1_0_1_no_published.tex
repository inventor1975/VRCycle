\begin{abstract}
Three classical set-theoretic themes --- the axiom of choice on
indistinguishable pairs (Russell's socks), the comparison of infinite
cardinals, and the uncountability of the continuum --- are re-read
operationally: an assertion counts only as an \emph{act}, performed and
witnessed, never as a completed object postulated into existence.  Under
this reading each theme splits cleanly in two, and both halves become
short machine-checked theorems.  For the socks: no selection \emph{rule}
exists --- no selector is at once blind to our labels and equivariant
under the swap, and the two premises are formal, so the statement
separates socks from shoes rather than proving the same of both; it is
the symmetry argument with its premises exhibited, not the
Fraenkel--Mostowski model-theoretic fact, which is cited and not
formalised.  Selection \emph{acts}, meanwhile, form a continuum
(the selectors are exactly the branches, which are not enumerable).  For
cardinals: a comparison is an act whose witness is data --- an explicit
injection $\N \hookrightarrow 2^{\N}$ is performed; the Cantor--Lawvere
diagonal is proved uniformly for every floor of the power-set ladder, on
the empty axiom list; the resulting order is \emph{partial by design},
since cardinal trichotomy is equivalent to full AC and is cited as a
formal-register label rather than claimed.  For uncountability: the sign
is flipped from prohibition to \emph{productivity} --- the fugitive from
any enumeration is computed by an explicit term, so the continuum is
productive in Post's sense: the catalogue that reads itself extends
itself.  Every proof is choice-free; the central results carry the empty
axiom list, verified by \texttt{\#print axioms}.  Nothing here is a new
classical theorem; the contribution is the operational re-reading, the
axiom pricing, and the machine check.

The paper is written to be \emph{verified from zero}: Section~\ref{sec:verify}
gives a single self-contained file (no mathlib, no imports) that reproves
every empty-axiom result in under a second, plus the full-corpus path and
one audit table, so that any agent --- human or machine --- can re-derive
every claim through the Lean~4 kernel without trusting the author or this
text.  An empty axiom list is precisely a verdict two parties who share
no axioms and no trust can both confirm: the strongest form of a
checkable claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: act against object}

The VR cycle reads mathematics operationally: an object is what an
operation reveals, an identity is earned by a witness, and nothing is
asserted on credit.  This note applies that discipline to the corner of
set theory where credit is most concentrated: choice, cardinal
comparison, and uncountability.

The organizing distinction is grammatical.  A \emph{rule} is a uniform
prescription that could be written down once and applied everywhere; an
\emph{act} is a performance, individuated by its occurrence, whose
witness exists after and because of it.  Classical set theory does not
mark this distinction: the axiom of choice postulates a completed system
of selections (an object), cardinal comparability postulates completed
comparisons, and uncountability is stated as a prohibition.  The
operational reading marks the distinction everywhere, and the three
classical themes then factor as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Choice.}  Where a rule exists, choice is available
  choice-free (dependent choice as recursion on a history-dependent
  rule; already in the cycle).  Where a rule provably cannot exist ---
  the indistinguishable pairs --- selection survives as an act; and acts
  are plentiful precisely where rules are impossible
  (Section~\ref{sec:socks}).
\item \textbf{Cardinals.}  A comparison of infinite types is an act:
  its witness (an injection) is data.  Performed comparisons yield a
  \emph{partial} order; the missing totality is not an oversight but a
  price tag, since trichotomy of cardinals is equivalent to full AC
  (Hartogs).  The ladder of power floors never closes from below, by a
  uniform diagonal (Section~\ref{sec:cardinals}).
\item \textbf{Uncountability.}  Read operationally it is not a wall but
  a generator: from every enumeration the escape is \emph{computed}.
  The continuum is productive (Section~\ref{sec:productive}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is not claimed.}  No classical theorem is contested, and
none is new: the mathematical content of each proof is elementary and
classical.  The axiom of choice is not refuted --- a symmetric-selector
impossibility is a statement about \emph{rules}, while AC postulates an
object exempt from symmetry.  Independence results (that ZF+DC proves
neither AC nor the Banach--Tarski decomposition; Solovay's model) are
metatheory: cited, never formalised here.  The contribution is the
re-reading, the split of each theme into an impossible-rule half and a
performed-act half, the axiom pricing of every step, and the machine
check.

\paragraph{The instrument.}  Everything is formalised over the cycle's
becoming-space: $\Branch := \N \to \mathsf{Bool}$, the space of
performed binary sequences, with its finite performed segments
(\texttt{Branch.take}).  The corpus is Lean~4 over mathlib; every public
object ends with \texttt{\#print axioms}.  ``Empty axiom list'' below
means exactly that: not even \texttt{propext}.

\section{Russell's socks: rules number zero, acts a continuum}
\label{sec:socks}

A countable family of pairs of indistinguishable socks admits no choice
rule: this is Russell's classical illustration, whose set-theoretic home
is the Fraenkel--Mostowski permutation models.  The operational
rendering: the two socks of box $k$ are \texttt{false}/\texttt{true} only
in the formal register's bookkeeping; the box itself offers no mark, so
the \emph{swap} at box $k$ is a symmetry of the situation.

\begin{thm}[\texttt{no\_symmetric\_selector}; empty axiom list]
No selection is swap-invariant beyond any finite bound: for every
selector $c : \N \to \mathsf{Bool}$ and every bound $N$ there is a box
$k \ge N$ at which the swap moves the selection.
\end{thm}

The proof is one line --- the swap at $k$ flips the pick at $k$, and no
Boolean equals its own negation.

\paragraph{That line is too cheap to carry the claim, and saying so is the
point.}  Read as \emph{``therefore no rule exists''}, the theorem above
proves too much: nothing in its statement mentions indistinguishability,
so the same text goes through for \emph{shoes}, where a rule plainly does
exist.  What separates the two cases --- that the swap is a symmetry of
the sock situation and not of the shoe situation --- was carried by the
prose of the preceding paragraph, not by the formalisation.  A
machine-checked claim whose load-bearing premise is editorial is exactly
the failure this cycle exists to catch, so it is corrected here rather
than defended.

Two definitions move the premise into the statement.  A
\emph{labelling} $l : \N \to \mathsf{Bool}$ records which element of each
pair we have named first; a \emph{selector} $s$ reads a labelling and
picks in every box.

\begin{defn}[the two premises, now formal]
$\mathrm{Equivariant}(s) := \forall l\,k,\; s(\mathrm{swap}_k l)(k) =
\lnot\, s(l)(k)$ --- renaming the two elements of pair $k$ carries the
pick with them, because it is the same object under a different name.
This is what ``indistinguishable'' means operationally.\\
$\mathrm{Blind}(s) := \forall l\,l'\,k,\; s(l)(k) = s(l')(k)$ --- the box
offers no mark, so no labelling of ours can enter the choice.
\end{defn}

\begin{thm}[\texttt{socks\_no\_rule}; empty axiom list]
No selector is both blind and equivariant.
\end{thm}

\begin{thm}[\texttt{shoes\_rule\_exists}, \texttt{shoes\_not\_blind};
empty axiom list]
$\mathrm{leftShoe} := \lambda l\,k.\, l(k)$ is equivariant and is not
blind.  A rule for shoes therefore exists and the impossibility does not
touch it.
\end{thm}

The second theorem is not decoration: without it the first would be an
impossibility proved about nothing in particular.  Together they say
where the obstruction lives --- in the collision of blindness with
equivariance, and nowhere else.  The depth is unchanged; the
\emph{discrimination} is what was missing.

\paragraph{What this is and is not.}  It is a machine-checked
formalisation of the symmetry argument, with its premises exhibited.  It
is \emph{not} Fraenkel--Mostowski: that the family genuinely has no
choice function in a permutation model is a statement about models,
which is cited here and formalised nowhere in this note.  The reader who
wants the model-theoretic fact should go to Fraenkel and Mostowski; what
is offered here is the operational grammar of the same obstruction.

What the classical discussion obscures is the other half:

\begin{thm}[\texttt{selectors\_not\_enumerable}; empty axiom list]
The selectors are exactly the branches; hence they are not enumerable:
no map $\N \to (\N \to \mathsf{Bool})$ is surjective.
\end{thm}

Selection over indistinguishable pairs therefore exists \emph{never as a
rule and uncountably as an act}: any performed sequence --- operationally,
a lawless coin --- selects.  Rules: zero.  Acts: a continuum.

\begin{rem}[The computational face]
The same split is a classical fact of distributed computing:
deterministic symmetric agents cannot break symmetry (leader election in
anonymous networks is deterministically impossible; Angluin 1980,
Itai--Rodeh 1981), while randomized agents succeed almost surely in
expected two rounds.  A companion simulation in the ZTL workspace
measures exactly this: deterministic twins never disagree; coin-equipped
socks break symmetry in $\approx 2$ rounds.  The deterministic half is
itself a theorem of the corpus (\texttt{twins\_never\_break},
\texttt{no\_unique\_leader}; empty axiom list): in an anonymous network
of identical automata started identically, the configuration is constant
across nodes at every round \emph{for arbitrary wiring} --- stronger
than a ring --- so no round distinguishes a unique node under any
predicate.  To our knowledge this folklore impossibility had not been
machine-checked before.  The sock chooses itself, or nothing chooses
it.
\end{rem}

\section{Cardinals as becomings}
\label{sec:cardinals}

\begin{defn}[Witnessed comparison]
$\mathrm{OpInj}(A,B) := \{ f : A \to B \mid f \text{ injective} \}$,
carried as \emph{data}: a performed comparison is an explicit injection,
not an existence claim.
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}[\texttt{natIntoBranch}; empty axiom list]
An explicit injection $\N \hookrightarrow \Branch$ is performed:
$n \mapsto$ the branch that fires exactly at $n$.
\end{prop}

\begin{thm}[\texttt{cantor\_ladder}; empty axiom list]
For every type $A$, no map $A \to (A \to \mathsf{Bool})$ is surjective.
\end{thm}

The diagonal is stated once and holds at \emph{every} floor: each
doubling --- each added colleague --- opens a floor the previous one
cannot reach.  Combining the two:

\begin{cor}[\texttt{nat\_strictly\_below\_branch}; empty axiom list]
$\N$ is operationally strictly below its power floor: the injection up
is performed, and no surjection comes back.
\end{cor}

\paragraph{The order is partial by design.}  Performed comparisons
compose, but no totality is claimed: trichotomy of cardinals is
equivalent to the full axiom of choice (Hartogs), i.e.\ to the completed
object this note declines.  Two becomings are comparable where a
comparison has been performed --- and nowhere else for free.  The
missing comparisons are not unknown truths; they are unperformed acts.

\section{Productive uncountability: the sign flipped}
\label{sec:productive}

Classically, uncountability is a prohibition: \emph{there is no}
enumeration.  Operationally the same content is a production:

\begin{defn}[\texttt{escape}]
$\mathrm{escape}(e) := n \mapsto \lnot\, e(n)(n)$, for any
$e : \N \to \Branch$.
\end{defn}

\begin{thm}[\texttt{escape\_escapes}, \texttt{branches\_productive};
empty axiom list]
For every enumeration $e$ and every $k$,
$\mathrm{escape}(e) \ne e(k)$; hence for every enumeration there
\emph{is} a branch it misses, and the witness is handed over, not merely
asserted.
\end{thm}

The fugitive is a \emph{term}: computed from the catalogue, differing
from each entry at that entry's own line.  This is productivity in
Post's sense, transplanted from computability theory to the
becoming-space: the continuum is not a large warehouse but a mirror ---
\emph{the catalogue that reads itself extends itself}.  Uncountability
is thereby re-signed from a wall to a generator: every attempt to
complete the listing manufactures the next item.  (In the cycle's
earlier ZTL expedition the same theme split into non-registrability and
incompleteness of registries, measured over marked streams; the present
theorem is the choice-free kernel of that split.)

\section{The demarcation: DC and the writable remainder}
\label{sec:dc}

The cycle already contains the positive half of choice: a
history-dependent rule determines a branch by recursion, choice-free
(\texttt{operational\_dependent\_choice}) --- dependent choice is the
\emph{performable} part of AC, the part with process semantics: each
selection is made in the context of those already made, and a witness
accrues step by step.  The present note adds the negative half (no rule
on symmetric pairs) and the act half (any branch selects).  What remains
of full AC above DC is precisely the part that can only be
\emph{written}: simultaneous selection over families no process
traverses.  That remainder buys the familiar exotica --- and, by
Solovay's model (metatheory, cited), giving it up costs analysis nothing
while dissolving, e.g., the Banach--Tarski decomposition, whose
non-measurable pieces are choice-certificates that can never be
exhibited.  In the cycle's terms: DC is choice earned in acts; the
remainder is choice on permanent credit.

\section{Findings}

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Rules zero, acts a continuum.}  Selection over
indistinguishable pairs is impossible as a rule (empty axiom list) and
uncountably plentiful as an act.  The two halves are one theorem apart
in the same file.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Comparison is an act; the order is partial by design.}
Trichotomy is not lost --- it is priced: it costs exactly full AC, and
the operational register declines the purchase.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}
\textbf{The ladder never closes from below}, uniformly and on the empty
axiom list: one diagonal serves every floor.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}
\textbf{Uncountability is a productivity.}  The positive form ---
witness handed over --- is strictly more informative than the classical
prohibition, and costs no axioms at all.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}
\textbf{The premise confession} (new in v1.0.1).  In v1.0.0 the sock
impossibility was machine-checked, and what the machine checked was that
no Boolean equals its own negation.  The premise that made it about
\emph{socks} --- that the swap is a symmetry of that situation and not of
the shoe situation --- sat in the prose.  The diagnostic is blunt and
worth keeping: \emph{the theorem could not tell socks from shoes}.  It is
repaired by moving both premises into the statement
(\texttt{socks\_no\_rule}) and exhibiting the case the statement must
spare (\texttt{shoes\_rule\_exists}, \texttt{shoes\_not\_blind}); the
mathematics is no deeper, the discrimination is what was added.  The
lesson generalises past this paper: an empty axiom list certifies the
step from premises to conclusion, and certifies \emph{nothing} about
whether the premises say what the surrounding sentences claim.  A
formalisation that proves the same theorem about the case it means to
exclude has not yet formalised the distinction it is about.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}
\textbf{The tier confession.}  The cycle's central diagonal
(\texttt{branches\_not\_enumerable}) had carried \texttt{[propext]}
since its first proof --- inherited not from the library but from our
own \texttt{simp} on a Boolean hypothesis.  One explicit
\texttt{Bool.noConfusion} restored the empty list.  The lesson is
methodological: before blaming the substrate, audit your own tactics.
\MEASURED{}: the current audit (Table~\ref{tab:audit}) shows the empty
list for every theorem named in this note save one --- DC, which carries
\texttt{[propext]}, marked and not load-bearing for the thesis.
\end{finding}

\section{Relation to the literature}

Fraenkel's and Mostowski's permutation models are the classical home of
the sock impossibility, and remain its only home here: this note
formalises the \emph{symmetry argument} over selections --- blindness
against equivariance, with a shoe exhibited to show the statement
discriminates --- and does not formalise the model-theoretic fact.  The
distinction is stated because v1.0.0 blurred it.  Post's productive sets supply the
name and the shape of Section~\ref{sec:productive}.  Angluin's and
Itai--Rodeh's results on anonymous leader election are the computational
face of the rule/act split.  The diagonal in the uniform form is
Lawvere's fixed-point argument specialised to $\mathsf{Bool}$.  Brouwer's
choice sequences stand behind the branch space and the lawless coin.
None of these results is claimed as new; the operational re-reading, the
splitting of each theme into rule-half and act-half, and the empty-list
machine check are the contribution.

\section{Machine verification: check it from zero}
\label{sec:verify}

This section is written so that any agent --- human or machine --- can
verify every claim of this paper \emph{without trusting the author, the
AI that helped write it, or this text.}  That is the point of the empty
axiom list.  A theorem whose \texttt{\#print axioms} is empty is provable
from nothing: two parties who share no axioms, no training, and no trust
can still both compute the same verdict, because the Lean~4 kernel
re-derives it from zero.  This is the strongest form of a zero-trust
claim, and it is mechanical, not rhetorical.

\paragraph{The fast path: one file, one command, no dependencies.}  Every
empty-axiom-list theorem of this paper is reproved, from scratch, in a
single self-contained file \texttt{Verify\_Choice\_standalone.lean} at the
repository root --- \emph{no mathlib, no lake, no imports}.  Any agent
checks the lot in under a second:
\begin{verbatim}
  lean Verify_Choice_standalone.lean
\end{verbatim}
It prints, for each of the thirteen theorems, \texttt{does not depend on any
axioms}.  This is the whole zero-trust claim in executable form: the
bare Lean~4 kernel (version in \texttt{lean-toolchain}, 4.29.1)
re-derives every verdict from nothing --- no library to trust, no author
to trust, no text to trust.  Two parties who share only the kernel can
both run it and agree.

\paragraph{The full path (with the substrate).}
\begin{verbatim}
  git clone https://github.com/inventor1975/VRCycle
  cd VRCycle          # tag choice-v1.0.0; toolchain in lean-toolchain (Lean 4.29.1)
  lake exe cache get  # mathlib cache
  lake build          # verifies the whole corpus (~8370 jobs)
\end{verbatim}
