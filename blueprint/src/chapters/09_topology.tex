\section{Position}
\label{sec:topology_position}

VR-Topology is the ninth and final work of the cycle.  It formalises
point-free topology in the style of Coquand~(1992) and Vickers~(2006),
proving a binary Tychonoff theorem with axiom profile
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]} --- no \texttt{Classical.choice}.

\textbf{Architectural principle.} The formal topology structure
$(\text{S},\,\text{le},\,\text{cov})$ is predicative: \texttt{cov}
lives in \texttt{Prop}, avoiding the universe-inflation problem that
blocked the original free-frame construction (Finding~T0).
The bridge to mathlib's impredactive \texttt{Order.Frame} is built
separately (Stage~7) and legitimately inherits \texttt{Classical.choice};
this does not affect the Tychonoff theorem.

\textbf{Unique status.} VR-Topology has no companion preprint by
curatorial decision.  This chapter is the canonical exposition of the
result.  It is also the chapter most directly relevant to Bas Spitters'
school (point-free topology, formal topology, constructive locales).

\textbf{Apparatus application.} \texttt{OperationalFormalTopology}
and \texttt{OperationalCompact} use \texttt{PredicateOperationality}
from Chapter~\ref{chap:apparatus}.  This is a fifth confirmation of
Finding~A3 (apparatus reuse without modification), extending the chain
AddGroup $\to$ Ring $\to$ Group $\to$ Module $\to$ \emph{FormalTopology}.

\textbf{`instProdOperationalCompact` is deferred.}  The abstract instance
for the product of two operational compact topologies is \emph{not} proved
in v1.0.0 due to a structural limitation in \texttt{listLowerOrder}
(Finding~T20).  Concrete instances on \texttt{Unit × Bool} work because
\texttt{IsOperational := True} on finite carriers.  Deferred to v1.1.0.

\section{FormalTopology structure}
\label{sec:topology_formal}

\begin{definition}[Common refinement]
\label{def:commonRefinement}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.commonRefinement}
\leanok
$\texttt{commonRefinement}\,\text{le}\,U\,V :=
\{c \mid \exists\,u \in U,\;\exists\,v \in V,\; \text{le}\,c\,u \land
\text{le}\,c\,v\}$.
The set of elements that refine some element of $U$ and some element of $V$.
Used in the \texttt{cov\_meet} axiom.
\axiomprofile{[]}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Formal topology]
\label{def:FormalTopology}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.FormalTopology}
\leanok
\uses{def:commonRefinement}
\texttt{FormalTopology} is a structure with fields:
\textbf{(i)}~\texttt{S : Type*} --- the carrier of basic elements;
\textbf{(ii)}~\texttt{le : S → S → Prop} --- a preorder (with
\texttt{le\_refl}, \texttt{le\_trans});
\textbf{(iii)}~\texttt{cov : S → Set S → Prop} --- the coverage
relation, satisfying five axioms:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{cov\_refl}: $a \in U \Rightarrow \texttt{cov}\,a\,U$ (membership implies coverage).
\item \texttt{cov\_trans}: if $\texttt{cov}\,a\,U$ and $\forall b \in U,\; \texttt{cov}\,b\,V$, then $\texttt{cov}\,a\,V$.
\item \texttt{cov\_ref\_mono}: if $\text{le}\,a\,b$ and $\texttt{cov}\,b\,U$, then $\texttt{cov}\,a\,U$.
\item \texttt{cov\_local}: if $\text{le}\,a\,b$ and $\texttt{cov}\,a\,U$, then $\texttt{cov}\,a\,\{c \in U \mid \text{le}\,c\,b\}$.
\item \texttt{cov\_meet} (Sambin extension, Finding~T7): if $\texttt{cov}\,a\,U$ and $\texttt{cov}\,a\,V$, then $\texttt{cov}\,a\,(\texttt{commonRefinement}\,\text{le}\,U\,V)$.
\end{itemize}
The first four axioms are Coquand~(1992); the fifth (\texttt{cov\_meet})
is the Sambin extension required for the product universal property.
\texttt{cov} lives in \texttt{Prop}: universe inflation is avoided.
\axiomprofile{[]}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inductively generated coverage]
\label{def:CoverGen}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.CoverGen}
\leanok
$\texttt{CoverGen}\,\text{le}\,\text{basicCov}$ is an inductive
\texttt{Prop} with six constructors mirroring the coverage axioms:
\texttt{basic} (import), \texttt{mem} (membership),
\texttt{trans} (transitivity), \texttt{ref\_mono} (refinement-monotonicity),
\texttt{local\_} (locality), \texttt{meet} (Sambin meet).
\texttt{Prop}-valued: no universe issue.  Structural induction on
\texttt{CoverGen} derivations is the key proof technique for Tychonoff.
\axiomprofile{[]}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Formal topology from a presentation]
\label{def:ofPresentation}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.FormalTopology.ofPresentation}
\leanok
\uses{def:FormalTopology, def:CoverGen}
$\texttt{FormalTopology.ofPresentation}$ assembles a \texttt{FormalTopology}
from a preorder and a relation of basic covers, using \texttt{CoverGen}
as the coverage relation.  This is the standard construction; concrete
formal topologies (Unit, Bool, Product) are built via this function.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Coverage monotonicity and singleton]
\label{thm:cov_utilities}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.FormalTopology.cov_mono, VRCycle.Topology.FormalTopology.cov_singleton,
      VRCycle.Topology.FormalTopology.cov_trans_singleton}
\leanok
\uses{def:FormalTopology}
$\texttt{cov\_mono}$: $U \subseteq V \Rightarrow \texttt{cov}\,a\,U
\Rightarrow \texttt{cov}\,a\,V$ (monotonicity).
$\texttt{cov\_singleton}$: $\texttt{cov}\,a\,\{a\}$ (every element
covers its own singleton).
$\texttt{cov\_trans\_singleton}$: transitivity via singleton.
All \axiomprofile{[]}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Binary Tychonoff}
\label{sec:topology_tychonoff}

\begin{definition}[\texttt{prodF} --- Vickers F-witness for the product]
\label{def:prodF}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.prodF, VRCycle.Topology.prodF_inhabited,
      VRCycle.Topology.prodF_upper_closed}
\leanok
\uses{def:CompactWitness, def:prod_topology}
$\texttt{prodF}\,T_1\,T_2\,w_1\,w_2$: a list $T$ is in \texttt{prodF}
iff for every decomposition of $T$ into parts $T_{1,\text{part}}$ and
$T_{2,\text{part}}$, either the first-coordinate projection of
$T_{1,\text{part}}$ is in $w_1.F$, or the second-coordinate projection
of $T_{2,\text{part}}$ is in $w_2.F$.
(Vickers 2006, Theorem~19 decomposition predicate.)
$\texttt{prodF\_inhabited}$: non-empty; proved by finite case dispatch
using \texttt{DecidableEq} (Finding~T16: decidability hypotheses
are required and explicit).
$\texttt{prodF\_upper\_closed}$: closed under \texttt{listLowerOrder}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{prodF\_cover\_closure}]
\label{thm:prodF_cover_closure}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.prodF_cover_closure,
      VRCycle.Topology.prodF_cover_closure_head}
\leanok
\uses{def:prodF, def:CoverGen, def:CompactWitness}
$\texttt{prodF\_cover\_closure}$: the \texttt{cover\_closure} field of
the \texttt{CompactWitness} built from \texttt{prodF}.
If $a$ is covered by a basic product cover, then the singleton $\{a\}$ is
in \texttt{prodF}.
This is the core technical lemma: structural induction on
\texttt{CoverGen} derivations, following Vickers~(2006).
The proof uses \texttt{cov\_meet} at the product level to handle
rectangular covers (the reason \texttt{cov\_meet} is load-bearing).
\axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[\texttt{tychonoff\_binary}]
\label{def:tychonoff_binary}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.tychonoff_binary}
\leanok
\uses{thm:prodF_cover_closure, def:CompactWitness, def:OperationalCompact}
$\texttt{tychonoff\_binary}\,(T_1\,T_2 : \texttt{FormalTopology})\,
[w_1 : \texttt{CompactWitness}\,T_1]\,[w_2 : \texttt{CompactWitness}\,T_2]
\,[\text{decidability instances}]$:
a \texttt{CompactWitness} for the product topology
$\texttt{FormalTopology.prod}\,T_1\,T_2$.

The compact witness for the binary product is constructed from the
Vickers F-witness \texttt{prodF}, \texttt{prodWitness} assembles the
four fields using \texttt{prodF\_cover\_closure} as the closure axiom.
This is a concrete \emph{def} (construction), not merely an existence
proof.

Proof sketch: structural induction on \texttt{CoverGen} derivations
(hence: Inductive Prop, no \texttt{Classical.choice}).  The
\texttt{ref\_mono} and \texttt{meet} cases use
\texttt{prodF\_upper\_closed} and product \texttt{cov\_meet}
respectively.  Decidability hypotheses (Finding~T16) are required for
constructive case splits in the \texttt{basic} case.

\noindent\axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]}.
Zero \texttt{Classical.choice}.  This is the primary claim of
VR-Topology: a constructive binary Tychonoff without the Axiom of Choice.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Saturated sets]
\label{def:SatSet}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.IsSaturated, VRCycle.Topology.SatSet,
      VRCycle.Topology.SatSet.saturate,
      VRCycle.Topology.SatSet.saturate_isSaturated}
\leanok
\uses{def:FormalTopology}
$\texttt{IsSaturated}\,T\,U := \forall a,\; T.\texttt{cov}\,a\,U \Rightarrow a \in U$:
a set is saturated iff it is downward-closed under coverage.
$\texttt{SatSet}\,T := \{U : \texttt{Set}\,T.S \mid \texttt{IsSaturated}\,T\,U\}$.
$\texttt{saturate}\,T\,U$: smallest saturated set containing $U$.
These define the frame elements.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Frame structure on \texttt{SatSet}]
\label{def:instFrame}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.instCompleteLattice,
      VRCycle.Topology.frameMinAx,
      VRCycle.Topology.instFrame}
\leanok
\uses{def:SatSet}
$\texttt{instCompleteLattice}$: \texttt{CompleteLattice (SatSet T)};
$\sup = \texttt{saturate}\,(\bigcup)$, $\inf = \bigcap$.
$\texttt{frameMinAx}$: \texttt{Order.Frame.MinimalAxioms (SatSet T)};
frame distributivity $a \sqcap \bigsqcup S = \bigsqcup (a \sqcap \cdot)\,''S$
proved via \texttt{iSup\_pos} bridging (Finding~T21).
$\texttt{instFrame}$: \texttt{Order.Frame (SatSet T)} derived from
\texttt{frameMinAx}.

\begin{remark}[Predicative meets impredicative at the bridge]
\label{rem:topology_bridge}
\leanok
Stages~1--6 (\texttt{FormalTopology} through \texttt{tychonoff\_binary})
are strict-constructive: axiom profile \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}.
Stage~7 crosses to mathlib's impredicative \texttt{Order.Frame}, which
legitimately acquires \texttt{Classical.choice} through the frame
typeclass machinery.  The bridge is expected and documented.
\texttt{instFrame} has \axiomprofile{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}.
The Tychonoff theorem (\texttt{tychonoff\_binary}) is unaffected:
it is proved before the bridge and sits in its own constructive stratum.
\end{remark}
\end{definition}

\section{Honest scope: deferred abstract instance}
\label{sec:topology_limitations}

\begin{remark}[\texttt{instProdOperationalCompact} deferred to v1.1.0]
\label{rem:prod_not_ready}
\notready
\uses{def:OperationalCompact, def:listLowerOrder}
The abstract instance
$\texttt{OperationalCompact}\,(\texttt{prod}\,T_1\,T_2)$ from
$[\texttt{OperationalCompact}\,T_1]\,[\texttt{OperationalCompact}\,T_2]$
is \emph{not} provided in v1.0.0.

Root cause (Finding~T20): \texttt{listLowerOrder} is one-directional
($\forall a \in A,\; \exists b \in B,\; \text{le}\,a\,b$), which
suffices for F-membership transfer (\texttt{prodF\_upper\_closed}) but
is insufficient to propagate per-element operationality from
\texttt{prodF} witnesses back to the product.  Specifically, elements
of the product cover may have no \texttt{prodLe}-preimage with
constrained operationality.

Concrete instances work: \texttt{Unit × Bool} satisfies
\texttt{OperationalCompact} trivially because \texttt{IsOperational :=
True} on finite carriers.  Abstract propagation is deferred to v1.1.0
(bidirectional \texttt{listLowerOrder} or restructured
\texttt{OperationalCompact}).  This is a scope discipline decision, not
a methodological failure.
\end{remark}

\section{Architectural amendments (T\_FINDINGS)}
\label{sec:topology_tfindings}

VR-Topology accumulated 18--19 architectural amendments (T0--T21) through
nine implementation sessions.  At no point did an architect direction error
propagate into committed Lean code; all were caught at the word-first or
paper-sketch phase.  The five patterns below document the methodological
significance.

\begin{remark}[Pattern~1 --- Architect direction errors caught at paper sketch]
\label{rem:T_pattern1}
\leanok
\uses{def:FormalTopology, def:CoverGen}
Findings T5, T8, T9, T11, T15, T17, T20: seven distinct architect
direction errors detected at the pre-implementation paper sketch stage
before any Lean code was committed.  This validates the
word-first/paper-sketch workflow as a recognition-discipline gate.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pattern~2 --- Classical inheritance from mathlib]
\label{rem:T_pattern2}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.FormalTopology.listLowerOrder,
      VRCycle.Topology.CompactWitness}
\leanok
\uses{def:listLowerOrder, def:CompactWitness}
Finding~T6 (\texttt{Nat.unpair\_pair} pulls \texttt{Classical.choice}
via \texttt{Nat.sqrt}): resolved by custom bit-interleaving pairing.
Finding~T13 (\texttt{Finset} transitively imports
\texttt{Classical.choice} via \texttt{Quot.unquot}): resolved by
replacing \texttt{Finset T.S} with \texttt{List T.S} throughout
\texttt{CompactWitness} (${\sim}268$ lines discarded, ${\sim}245$
rewritten).  The strict constructive baseline requires careful
verification of all mathlib import chains.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pattern~3 --- Under-specification requiring explicit axiomatisation]
\label{rem:T_pattern3}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.FormalTopology}
\leanok
\uses{def:FormalTopology}
Finding~T7 (\texttt{cov\_meet} required for product universal property),
Finding~T14 (\texttt{opProdBasicCov} layer needed),
Finding~T16 (decidability hypotheses in \texttt{tychonoff\_binary}
signature), Finding~T19 (\texttt{op\_preserved\_by\_le} field required).
All were implicit in architect plans and surfaced as explicit requirements
during implementation.  Adding \texttt{cov\_meet} to \texttt{FormalTopology}
was the most architecturally significant (changed the structure definition
globally).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pattern~4 --- Notation/elaboration bridging]
\label{rem:T_pattern4}
\lean{VRCycle.Topology.instFrame}
\leanok
\uses{def:instFrame}
Finding~T21 (\texttt{iSup\_pos} bridging): mathlib's notation
$\bigsqcup_{b \in S} A \sqcap b$ elaborates to nested \texttt{iSup}
with conditional; bridging required identifying the specific lemma
\texttt{iSup\_pos}.  This is a notation/elaboration finding, not
a mathematical one.  The paper proof closes; Lean's infrastructure
requires a specific bridge tactic.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pattern~5 --- Scope discipline under fatigue]
\label{rem:T_pattern5}
\leanok
\uses{rem:prod_not_ready}
Finding~T20 (R3 resolution): when the abstract
\texttt{instProdOperationalCompact} could not be proved due to the
\texttt{listLowerOrder} directionality issue, the correct response was
to provide concrete-only instances and defer the abstract case to
v1.1.0, rather than forcing an incorrect proof or adding a
\texttt{sorry}.  This preserved cycle stability and honesty.
\end{remark}

\section{Position relative to existing work}
\label{sec:topology_comparison}

\begin{remark}[Vickers (2006)]
\label{rem:compare_vickers}
The proof of \texttt{tychonoff\_binary} follows Vickers's
``Compactness in locales and in formal topology'' (2006, Theorem~19).
The key adaptation is the translation to Lean~4's inductive type theory:
the classical Kuratowski-finite sets are replaced by
\texttt{List}-encoded covers (Finding~T13); the classical case splits
become explicit \texttt{Decidable} hypotheses (Finding~T16).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Coquand (1992) and Sambin]
\label{rem:compare_coquand}
The coverage axioms follow Coquand~(1992)'s presentation.  The fifth
axiom (\texttt{cov\_meet}) is the Sambin extension, required for the
product universal property (Finding~T7).  Coquand's minimal four-axiom
system was insufficient; Sambin's meet axiom is indispensable.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Tosun and pointless localic algebra]
\label{rem:compare_tosun}
Tosun's Agda formalisation of locale theory covers Stone and spectral
spaces in point-free settings.  VR-Topology uses formal topology
(coverage relations) rather than frames directly; the bridge to
\texttt{Order.Frame} (Stage~7) connects the two frameworks.
The choice of formal topology (not frames) is what enables the
constructive axiom profile of \texttt{tychonoff\_binary}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Spitters and pointless schemes]
\label{rem:compare_spitters}
Spitters's programme in pointless algebraic geometry (Grothendieck
toposes in point-free formulation, topos of sheaves on a locale) is
the downstream application context for formal topology.  VR-Topology
provides a verified binary Tychonoff in the style that formal topology
researchers expect.  The bridge to \texttt{Order.Frame} (Sections~\ref{sec:topology_bridge})
prepares the infrastructure for sheaf-theoretic applications.
\end{remark}
