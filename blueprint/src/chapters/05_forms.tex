\section{Position}
\label{sec:forms_position}

VR-Forms is the two-register apparatus over VR-Sets.  The \emph{operational
register} (L\textsubscript{0}) describes operational sets in the sense of
Chapter~\ref{chap:sets}: objects built from $\emptyset$ by the closure
principle.  The \emph{formal register} (L\textsubscript{1}) accommodates
syntactic descriptions without operational obligation---classically
uncountable objects, paradoxical classes, and any correctly built term
regardless of operationality or consistency.  The two registers are connected
by the translation $\pi$ and by Theorem~III.1 (conservativity).

\textbf{Clarification on register language.} The registers describe modes
of description, not separate operational levels.  All descriptions are
operational acts; the distinction is whether the described referent has an
operational correlate.

\textbf{Architectural note.} The Lean formalisation is a \emph{shallow
embedding} over mathlib.  Formal terms are Lean strings carrying a register
tag; $\pi$ is a total Lean function; realisability is a Lean predicate.
The apparatus has one explicit structural boundary: Theorem~III.1
(conservativity) is proved in the preprint but not formalised in Lean.
Full formalisation would require deep-embedded \texttt{Formula~L₀},
\texttt{Derivation~T₀}, \texttt{Derivation~T₁} types and an induction proof
over derivations --- a proof-theory project larger than the entire VR-Sets
Lean cycle.  This boundary is not a defect; it is the premeditated
stopping point of the shallow embedding.  See \S\,\ref{subsec:conservativity}.

\noindent\axiomprofile{[]} for 3 Stage~1 objects; \axiomprofile{[propext,
Quot.sound]} for all remaining; zero \texttt{Classical.choice} in the
entire cycle.

\section{Realisability (Part II \S\,II.7)}
\label{sec:forms_realisable}

\begin{definition}[Operational realisability predicate]
\label{def:isRealisable}
\lean{VR.Forms.isRealisable}
\leanok
\uses{def:FormalTerm, def:OSet}
$\texttt{isRealisable} : \texttt{FormalTerm} \to \mathrm{Prop}$ is a
total \texttt{def} function defined by \texttt{match} on the description
string:

\begin{tabular}{ll}
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"∅"}$\urcorner$ & $\exists\, s : \mathrm{OSet},\; \forall x,\; x \notin s$ \\
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"omega\_OSet"}$\urcorner$ & $\exists\, s : \mathrm{OSet},\; \emptyset \in s \land \forall n \in s,\; \texttt{insert}\,n\,n \in s$ \\
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"osetPair"}$\urcorner$ & $\forall a\,b : \mathrm{OSet},\; \exists\, s : \mathrm{OSet},\; \forall x,\; x \in s \leftrightarrow x=a \lor x=b$ \\
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"Conjecture\_IV\_1\_Statement"}$\urcorner$ & \texttt{VR.Sets.Conjecture\_IV\_1\_Statement} \\
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"Conjecture\_IV\_2\_Statement"}$\urcorner$ & \texttt{VR.Sets.Conjecture\_IV\_2\_Statement} \\
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"AFA\_Statement"}$\urcorner$ & \texttt{VR.Sets.AFA\_Statement} \\
any other & \texttt{False}
\end{tabular}

Each realisable case maps to an existential Lean Prop (not \texttt{True}),
preserving the mathematical content of the witness.
\end{definition}

$\texttt{isRealisable\_osetPair}$: via \texttt{osetPair}.

\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Three-category structure of formal terms]
\label{rem:three_category}
\lean{VR.Forms.isRealisable}
\leanok
\uses{def:isRealisable, def:Conjecture_IV_1, def:Conjecture_IV_2,
      def:AFA_Statement}
The \texttt{isRealisable} match encodes a triadic classification:
(a)~\emph{Provably realisable} ---
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"∅"}$\urcorner$, $\ulcorner$\texttt{"omega\_OSet"}$\urcorner$,

(b)~\emph{Open realisability} ---
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"Conjecture\_IV\_X"}$\urcorner$: realisability is

(c)~\emph{Provably non-realisable} --- split into trivially-False (catch-all,

\texttt{AFA\_Refuted}).
\end{remark}

\section{Transit (Part III)}
\label{sec:forms_transit}

\begin{definition}[Translation $\pi$ (shallow)]
\label{def:translate_pi}
\lean{VR.Forms.translate_pi}
\leanok
\uses{def:FormalTerm, def:osetEmpty, def:set_ops}
$\texttt{translate\_pi} : \texttt{FormalTerm} \to \mathrm{Prop}$ is the
\emph{specific} layer: for each named formal term, the direct operational
predicate naming a concrete VR-Sets object.  For $\ulcorner$\texttt{"∅"}$\urcorner$:
$\forall x,\; x \notin \texttt{osetEmpty}$ (not an existential).
For $\ulcorner$\texttt{"omega\_OSet"}$\urcorner$: $\emptyset \in
\texttt{omega\_OSet} \land \ldots$.  Catch-all: \texttt{False}.
Contrast with \texttt{isRealisable} (\emph{existential} layer).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[π-translation lemmas]
\label{thm:translate_pi_base}
\lean{VR.Forms.translate_pi_empty, VR.Forms.translate_pi_omega,
      VR.Forms.translate_pi_osetPair}
\leanok
\uses{def:translate_pi, def:osetEmpty, def:set_ops}
Direct proofs for $\ulcorner$\texttt{"∅"}$\urcorner$,
$\ulcorner$\texttt{"omega\_OSet"}$\urcorner$, $\ulcorner$\texttt{"osetPair"}$\urcorner$
by the VR-Sets theorems.  All at \axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Transit: $\pi$ implies realisability]
\label{thm:translate_implies_realisable}
\lean{VR.Forms.translate_implies_realisable}
\leanok
\uses{def:translate_pi, def:isRealisable}
$\forall t : \texttt{FormalTerm},\;
\texttt{translate\_pi}(t) \Rightarrow \texttt{isRealisable}(t)$.
Proved by \texttt{by\_cases} discrimination on the named constructors
(possible since \texttt{DecidableEq FormalTerm}) followed by existential
introduction with the concrete named witness.  The \emph{converse does
not hold}: from $\exists\, s,\; \ldots$ one cannot recover the specific
named object without Skolemisation.  The transit pattern operates
exclusively in the forward direction.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The conservativity boundary (Theorem~III.1)}
\label{subsec:conservativity}

\begin{theorem}[Conservativity of $T_1$ over $T_0$]
\label{thm:conservativity}
\notready
\uses{def:translate_pi, def:isRealisable}
\textbf{Theorem III.1} (preprint §\,III.2, verbatim): «The theory $T_1$
(VR-Forms) is conservative over $T_0$ (VR-Sets) in the operational
register: any formula $\varphi \in L_0$ that is derivable in $T_1$ is
already derivable in $T_0$.  Equivalently: formal terms do not produce
new operational theorems.»

The theorem is proved in the preprint via induction on derivations through
the translation $\pi$.  It is \textbf{not formalised} in the Lean cycle.

\begin{remark}[Why \notready]
\label{rem:conservativity_boundary}
Full formalisation requires deep-embedded types:
$\texttt{Formula}\,L_0$, $\texttt{Formula}\,L_1$,
$\texttt{Derivation}\,T_0$, $\texttt{Derivation}\,T_1$, and an
induction proof over the structure of $\pi$.  This is a proof-theory
project in its own right, not within the shallow-embedding depth chosen
for the VR cycle.  The boundary is premeditated, documented in the
Lean source of \texttt{Transit.lean}, and honest: conservativity is
\emph{mathematically proved} (preprint §\,III.2) but
\emph{Lean-unformalisable} at shallow depth.  It is not an open
conjecture; it is a proved result whose Lean formalisation lies outside
the scope of this cycle.  See Observation~3 (\S\,\ref{sec:forms_boundary}).
\end{remark}
\end{theorem}

\section{Bridge theorems (Part V)}
\label{sec:forms_bridge}

\begin{theorem}[Negative bridge: AFA is non-realisable]
\label{thm:bridge_AFA}
\lean{VR.Forms.bridge_AFA}
\leanok
\uses{def:isRealisable, thm:AFA_Refuted}
$\lnot\,\texttt{isRealisable}\,\ulcorner$\texttt{"AFA\_Statement"}$\urcorner$.
Proof: \texttt{isRealisable} reduces definitionally to
\texttt{VR.Sets.AFA\_Statement}; the theorem is exactly
\texttt{AFA\_Refuted} from Chapter~\ref{chap:sets}.

This is the first theorem in VR-Forms using a VR-Sets
\emph{refutation} (not a VR-Sets existence theorem).  The cross-cycle
chain: \texttt{AFA\_Refuted} (Ch.~\ref{chap:sets})
$\to$ \texttt{bridge\_AFA}.
\axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Open conditional bridges: Conjectures~IV.1 and IV.2]
\label{thm:bridge_Conjectures}
\lean{VR.Forms.bridge_Conjecture_IV_1, VR.Forms.bridge_Conjecture_IV_2}
\leanok
\uses{def:isRealisable, def:Conjecture_IV_1, def:Conjecture_IV_2}
$\texttt{isRealisable}\,\ulcorner$\texttt{"Conjecture\_IV\_1\_Statement"}$\urcorner
\leftrightarrow \texttt{Conjecture\_IV\_1\_Statement}$.
$\texttt{isRealisable}\,\ulcorner$\texttt{"Conjecture\_IV\_2\_Statement"}$\urcorner
\leftrightarrow \texttt{Conjecture\_IV\_2\_Statement}$.

Both iff proofs are $\langle\texttt{id},\texttt{id}\rangle$ (definitional
equality); the mathematical \emph{content} is open.  The bridge theorem
is trivially proved; its resolution requires proving or refuting the
corresponding VR-Sets conjecture.  Status: open realisability
(category~(b) of the triadic structure).
\end{theorem}

\section{Substrate (Stage 6)}

\begin{definition}[Carrier and operational substrate]
\label{def:Carrier}
\lean{VR.Forms.Carrier, VR.Forms.Operational}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet, def:FormalTerm}
$\texttt{Carrier}$ is an inductive type with two constructors:
$\texttt{.obj} : \mathrm{OSet}.{0} \to \texttt{Carrier}$ (operational
objects in the operational register) and
$\texttt{.term} : \texttt{FormalTerm} \to \texttt{Carrier}$ (formal terms).

$\texttt{Operational} : \texttt{Carrier} \to \mathrm{Prop}$ by:
$\texttt{Operational}(\texttt{.obj}\,a) := \texttt{Acc}({\cdot}\in{\cdot})\,a$
(well-foundedness; content-ful via \texttt{ZFSet.mem\_wf});
$\texttt{Operational}(\texttt{.term}\,\_) := \texttt{True}$
(by the Principle of Forms: any inscribed formal term carries substrate
unconditionally).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Substrate totality and base cases]
\label{thm:operational_total}
\lean{VR.Forms.operational_total, VR.Forms.operational_empty,
      VR.Forms.omega_substrate}
\leanok
\uses{def:Carrier, def:osetEmpty, def:set_ops}
$\texttt{operational\_total}$: $\forall c : \texttt{Carrier},\;
\texttt{Operational}(c)$.  Proof: \texttt{.obj}~branch via
\texttt{ZFSet.mem\_wf.apply} (Classical-free);
\texttt{.term}~branch by \texttt{trivial}.

$\texttt{operational\_empty}$: $\texttt{Operational}(\texttt{.obj}\,
\texttt{osetEmpty})$ --- the primordial object $\emptyset$ carries
operational substrate.

$\texttt{omega\_substrate}$: $\texttt{Operational}(\texttt{.obj}\,
\texttt{omega\_OSet}) \land \texttt{Operational}(\texttt{.term}\,
\ulcorner$\texttt{"omega\_OSet"}$\urcorner)$ --- $\omega$ carries
substrate in both registers, connecting Stage~6 to
\texttt{mixed\_omega\_two\_register}.

\noindent\axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]} for all three;
zero \texttt{Classical.choice}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Conservativity boundary as structural feature (\S\,IX.2)}
\label{sec:forms_boundary}

VR-Forms Lean has \emph{one} explicit structural boundary (at conservativity),
in contrast to VR-Sets Lean's five mathematical-content boundaries.  The
single boundary has four substructural manifestations in the formalisation.

\begin{remark}[Observation~3 --- Conservativity is the explicit boundary]
\label{rem:obs3_boundary}
\lean{VR.Forms.translate_pi}
\leanok
\uses{thm:translate_implies_realisable}
Theorem~III.1 is mathematically proved in the preprint (full inductive
proof via $\pi$) but is Lean-unformalisable at shallow-embedding depth.
The Lean cycle documents this by verbatim citation in the doc-comment of
\texttt{Transit.lean} and does not introduce
\texttt{def Conjecture\_Conservativity : Prop} --- which would misrepresent
a proved result as an open conjecture.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Observation~4 --- Equation-compiler catch-all does not reduce]
\label{rem:obs4_catchall}
\lean{VR.Forms.translate_implies_realisable}
\leanok
\uses{thm:translate_implies_realisable}
The catch-all \texttt{|\,\_ => False} in \texttt{translate\_pi} does not
reduce in term mode for a schematic variable \texttt{x✝}.  The zero-axiom
fix: \texttt{by\_cases} discrimination enabled by \texttt{DecidableEq
FormalTerm}, followed by \texttt{unfold + split + simp\_all
[FormalTerm.mk.injEq]} in the residual branch.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Observation~5 --- Realisability and $\pi$-translation are complementary layers]
\label{rem:obs5_layers}
\lean{VR.Forms.isRealisable, VR.Forms.translate_pi,
      VR.Forms.translate_implies_realisable}
\leanok
\uses{def:isRealisable, def:translate_pi, thm:translate_implies_realisable}
\texttt{isRealisable} (existential: $\exists$ witness) and
\texttt{translate\_pi} (specific: named concrete object) form two
complementary layers connected by \texttt{translate\_implies\_realisable}
via existential introduction.  The converse (specific $\Leftarrow$
existential) is not provable: Skolemisation is unavailable from the
existential in the shallow embedding.  The transit pattern of §\,IV.2
operates exclusively in the forward direction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Observation~6 --- Two-level structure of negative cases]
\label{rem:obs6_negative}
\lean{VR.Forms.not_isRealisable_Russell, VR.Forms.bridge_AFA}
\leanok
\uses{thm:not_realisable, thm:bridge_AFA}
Two levels of non-realisability:
\emph{Level~1 (trivially False)}: Russell, Vitali, classical~$\mathbb{R}$,
classical~$\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ --- catch-all redirects to \texttt{False},
proof is \texttt{id}, no VR-Sets theorem needed.
\emph{Level~2 (VR-Sets-refutable)}: $\ulcorner$\texttt{AFA\_Statement}$\urcorner$
--- the match returns a mathematically formulated \texttt{Prop};
non-realisability requires \texttt{AFA\_Refuted} from VR-Sets.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Observation~7 --- Three-category structure mirrors VR-Sets tier structure]
\label{rem:obs7_triad}
\lean{VR.Forms.bridge_AFA, VR.Forms.bridge_Conjecture_IV_1,
      VR.Forms.isRealisable_empty}
\leanok
\uses{thm:isRealisable_base, thm:bridge_AFA, thm:bridge_Conjectures}
The triadic classification of formal terms (provably realisable~/ open ~/
provably non-realisable) parallels VR-Sets Stage~11's three-tier
formalisation result (proved theorems / refuted claims / open formulations),
but localised at the level of formal terms within the formal register.
\end{remark}

\section{Methodological observations (Part IX)}
\label{sec:forms_method}

\subsection*{\S\,IX.1 Foundation-level properties}

\begin{remark}[Observation~1 --- Foundation file is import-free and axiom-free]
\label{rem:obs1_import}
\lean{VR.Forms.Register, VR.Forms.FormalTerm}
\leanok
\uses{def:Register, def:FormalTerm}
\texttt{Language.lean} introduces the syntactic skeleton without importing
any mathlib or VR-Sets module.  \texttt{Register} and \texttt{FormalTerm}
are axiom-free.  This contrasts with VR-Numbers (imports mathlib's natural
number infrastructure) and VR-Sets (imports mathlib's \texttt{ZFSet},
inheriting \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}).  The base layer of VR-Forms is
thus axiom-free \texttt{[]}, whereas the base layers of VR-Numbers and
VR-Sets inherit logical axioms from their mathlib imports.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Observation~2 --- Realisability inherits Classical-free closure of VR-Sets]
\label{rem:obs2_classical}
\lean{VR.Forms.isRealisable_empty, VR.Forms.isRealisable_omega,
      VR.Forms.isRealisable_osetPair}
\leanok
\uses{thm:isRealisable_base}
The three base realisable cases correspond to the Classical-free closure
theorems of VR-Sets (\texttt{osetEmpty}, \texttt{omega\_OSet},
\texttt{osetPair}) --- all at \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}, none
requiring \texttt{Classical.choice}.  Realisable terms tied to Classical
VR-Sets theorems (replacement, choice, foundation) would inherit
\texttt{Classical.choice}; no such terms appear in this formalisation.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{\S\,IX.3 Structural patterns}

\begin{remark}[Observation~8 --- Universe handling across cross-cycle boundaries]
\label{rem:obs8_universe}
\lean{VR.Forms.isRealisable, VR.Forms.mixed_omega_two_register}
\leanok
\uses{def:isRealisable, thm:mixed_omega}
Two universe-management issues arose from the cross-cycle nature of
VR-Forms.  (a)~Stage~2: \texttt{OSet.\{0\}} must be pinned explicitly in
\texttt{isRealisable}; without it Lean cannot infer the universe inside
\texttt{namespace VR.Forms}.  (b)~Stage~5: projecting \texttt{.1} from
\texttt{Theorem\_III\_6\_Infinity} (two-universe signature \texttt{\{u v\}})
generates metavariables; the fix is \texttt{translate\_pi\_omega.1}
(universe pinned in the match case).  Both reflect the same architectural
fact: VR-Forms is a cross-cycle module, and universe inference does not
propagate across cycle boundaries without explicit annotation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Observation~9 --- ZFA boundary in both registers simultaneously]
\label{rem:obs9_zfa}
\lean{VR.Forms.mixed_AFA_boundary}
\leanok
\uses{thm:mixed_AFA_boundary}
\texttt{mixed\_AFA\_boundary} formalises the parallel manifestation of
well-foundedness across both registers in a single Lean \texttt{Prop}.
What appeared in VR-Sets Part~X as Boundary~B.5 (``ZFA total absence'')
appears in VR-Forms Lean as a theorem with cross-register content: the
same structural fact, now expressed in the two-register apparatus
VR-Forms provides for talking about both registers simultaneously.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{\S\,IX.4 Cross-cycle integration}

\begin{remark}[Observation~10 --- Zero \texttt{Classical.choice} across the entire cycle]
\label{rem:obs10_zero_classical}
\lean{VR.Forms.bridge_AFA, VR.Forms.mixed_AFA_boundary}
\leanok
The cycle plan predicted \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}
or stricter.  The actual result: 3 axiom-free objects (Stage~1), 15+ at
\texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]} (Stages~2--6), zero requiring
\texttt{Classical.choice}.  The structural reason: realisable cases
correspond to the Classical-free closure layer of VR-Sets; non-realisable
cases either reduce to \texttt{False} (\texttt{id}) or use
\texttt{AFA\_Refuted} (Classical-free via inductive \texttt{PSet}).
VR-Forms reaches \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]} with zero
\texttt{Classical.choice} throughout, whereas VR-Numbers, VR-Sets and
VR-Sets-ZFA each reach the full
\texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} ceiling.
\end{remark}

\section{Axiom profile}
\label{sec:forms_axiom_profile}

\axiomprofile{[]} for 3 Stage~1 objects:
\texttt{Register}, \texttt{FormalTerm}, and the \texttt{⌜·⌝} notation.
Language.lean imports nothing; pure Lean~4 prelude.

\axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]} for all remaining public objects
(Stages~2--6), including all realisability lemmas, $\pi$-translation
theorems, bridge theorems, examples, mixed formulas, and substrate results.
\textbf{Zero \texttt{Classical.choice}.}

Comparison with VR-Sets (Chapter~\ref{chap:sets}):
VR-Sets has 6 objects at the full ceiling
\texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} through four structurally
distinct mechanisms (ordinal-valued constructions, definability, foundation,
choice).  VR-Forms has none.  VR-Forms achieves axiom-minimality by staying
entirely on the shallow side of its single structural boundary;
VR-Sets achieves it by navigating five mathematical-content boundaries.

VR-Forms surfaces \emph{one} structural boundary (conservativity, at the
proof-theoretic meta-level) with four substructural manifestations.
VR-Sets surfaces five boundaries at the mathematical-content level.
Both are honest treatments of their respective limits.
