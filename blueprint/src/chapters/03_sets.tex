\section{Position}
\label{sec:sets_position}

VR-Sets is the operational theory of sets built from the single primitive
$\emptyset$.  A set is not a container but an operational functionality:
upon a query, it either reveals an element (which is itself a set) or
reveals nothing.  The relation $x \in y$ is read as a reference---the
functionality of $y$ uses $x$ in its description---not as physical
containment.  The closure principle (§\,II.3 of the preprint) replaces
the ZF axioms of existence: every operationally describable functionality
is a set.  Individual closure theorems (Pairing, Union, Power, Infinity,
Replacement, Choice) are proved, not postulated.

\textbf{Architectural load-bearing fact.} The implementation is
$\texttt{OSet} := \texttt{ZFSet}$ --- an \texttt{abbrev} (reducible
definition) over mathlib's quotient $\texttt{ZFSet} := \texttt{Quotient
PSet.setoid}$.  VR-Sets is built \emph{on} mathlib's ZFSet, not in a
parallel universe.  All nine ZFC axioms are theorems on this type; no
extra axioms are introduced.  The ZFA-mode cannot be modelled in
\texttt{ZFSet} (cyclic sets are provably absent); this structural
boundary is formalized as \texttt{AFA\_Refuted} and
\texttt{quineAtom\_impossible}, both axiom-free.

\section{Foundation (Part II)}
\label{sec:sets_foundation}

\begin{definition}[$\mathrm{OSet}$ --- the type of operational sets]
\label{def:OSet}
\lean{VR.Sets.OSet}
\leanok
$\texttt{OSet} := \texttt{ZFSet}$ (declared as \texttt{abbrev}, hence
reducible).
\texttt{ZFSet} is \texttt{Quotient PSet.setoid} where $\texttt{PSet}
:= \texttt{inductive mk}(\alpha : \texttt{Type}, A : \alpha \to
\texttt{PSet})$.  The pre-set constructor $\texttt{mk}(\alpha, A)$
encodes Definition~1 directly: $\alpha$ is the index of queries that
produce an element, $A$ is the response function.  The quotient by
$\texttt{PSet.Equiv}$ (bisimulation) is the operational identity
$\equiv$ of Definition~4, collapsed to Lean's $=$.
Because \texttt{OSet} is an \texttt{abbrev}, all mathlib operations on
\texttt{ZFSet} (\texttt{sUnion}, \texttt{powerset}, \texttt{image},
\ldots) apply to \texttt{OSet} without wrappers.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Empty set and operational identity]
\label{def:osetEmpty}
\lean{VR.Sets.osetEmpty}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
$\texttt{osetEmpty} := \emptyset : \texttt{OSet}$ via the
\texttt{EmptyCollection ZFSet} instance.  Operational identity
$a \equiv b$ is notation for $a = b : \texttt{OSet}$, so
$\texttt{PSet.Equiv}$ is definitionally absorbed into Lean's $=$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lemma~II.1 --- Extensionality]
\label{thm:Lemma_II_1}
\lean{VR.Sets.Lemma_II_1_Extensionality}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
If $\forall x : \mathrm{OSet},\; x \in a \leftrightarrow x \in b$,
then $a \equiv b$.
Proved by \texttt{ZFSet.ext}: the membership condition is exactly
Definition~4's coincidence of functionalities.  The preprint's remark
that «extensionality is built into the definition» is confirmed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Lemma~II.2 --- Uniqueness of $\emptyset$]
\label{thm:Lemma_II_2}
\lean{VR.Sets.Lemma_II_2_UniquenessEmpty}
\leanok
\uses{def:osetEmpty, thm:Lemma_II_1}
$\exists!\, a : \mathrm{OSet},\; \forall x,\; x \notin a$.
Existence: \texttt{osetEmpty}.  Uniqueness: two sets with no members
have the same membership, hence are equal by Lemma~II.1.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Operational depth]
\label{def:operationalDepth}
\lean{VR.Sets.operationalDepth}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
$\texttt{operationalDepth}(a) := \texttt{ZFSet.rank}(a) : \mathrm{Ordinal}$
--- the Von Neumann rank of the membership tree.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lemma~II.3 --- Operational depth]
\label{thm:Lemma_II_3}
\lean{VR.Sets.Lemma_II_3_DepthEmpty, VR.Sets.Lemma_II_3_DepthMono}
\leanok
\uses{def:operationalDepth, def:osetEmpty}
$\texttt{operationalDepth}(\emptyset) = 0$ (\texttt{rank\_empty}).
If $b \in a$ then $\texttt{operationalDepth}(b) <
\texttt{operationalDepth}(a)$ (\texttt{rank\_lt\_of\_mem}).  Depth is
strictly monotone on membership, providing the induction instrument
for VR-Sets (the analogue of A4 lifted to sets).

The cyclic case (Quine atom) is absent from \texttt{OSet} by
construction: \texttt{PSet} is inductive, so all elements are accessible.
\end{theorem}

\section{ZFC axioms as closure theorems (Part III)}
\label{sec:sets_zfc}

\begin{definition}[Set-theoretic operations]
\label{def:set_ops}
\lean{VR.Sets.osetPair, VR.Sets.osetUnion, VR.Sets.osetPower,
      VR.Sets.omega_OSet, VR.Sets.osetReplacement}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
$\texttt{osetPair}(a,b) := \{a,b\}$ (unordered pair via \texttt{Insert
ZFSet ZFSet}).
$\texttt{osetUnion}(a) := \texttt{ZFSet.sUnion}(a)$ ($\bigcup a$).
$\texttt{osetPower}(a) := \texttt{ZFSet.powerset}(a)$ ($\mathcal{P}(a)$).
$\omega_{\texttt{OSet}} := \texttt{ZFSet.omega}$ (first infinite Von
Neumann ordinal).
$\texttt{osetReplacement}(F, a) := @\texttt{ZFSet.image}\,F\,
(\texttt{Classical.allZFSetDefinable}\,\_)\,a$ (image of $a$ under $F$;
\texttt{noncomputable}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~III.3 --- Pairing]
\label{thm:T_III_3}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_3_Pairing}
\leanok
\uses{def:set_ops}
$\forall x,\; x \in \{a,b\} \leftrightarrow x = a \lor x = b$.
Proved by \texttt{ZFSet.mem\_pair}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~III.4 --- Union]
\label{thm:T_III_4}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_4_Union}
\leanok
\uses{def:set_ops}
$\forall x,\; x \in \bigcup a \leftrightarrow \exists\, c \in a,\; x \in c$.
Proved by \texttt{ZFSet.mem\_sUnion}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~III.5 --- Power set]
\label{thm:T_III_5}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_5_Power}
\leanok
\uses{def:set_ops}
$\forall x,\; x \in \mathcal{P}(a) \leftrightarrow x \subseteq a$.
Proved by \texttt{ZFSet.mem\_powerset}.

\emph{First structural boundary}: Lean's $\mathcal{P}(a)$ admits all
subsets (classical); the preprint's $\mathcal{P}(a)$ contains only
operationally describable subsets (countably many for $a = \omega$).
The countability of the operational $\mathcal{P}(\omega)$ is
metatheoretic.  See Observation~B.1.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~III.6 --- Infinity]
\label{thm:T_III_6_inf}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_6_Infinity_Zero, VR.Sets.Theorem_III_6_Infinity_Succ,
      VR.Sets.Theorem_III_6_Infinity}
\leanok
\uses{def:set_ops}
$\emptyset \in \omega_{\texttt{OSet}}$ (\texttt{omega\_zero}) and
$n \in \omega_{\texttt{OSet}} \Rightarrow \texttt{insert}\,n\,n \in
\omega_{\texttt{OSet}}$ (\texttt{omega\_succ}).  The conjunction is
\texttt{Theorem\_III\_6\_Infinity}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~III.7 --- Replacement]
\label{thm:T_III_7}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_7_Replacement}
\leanok
\uses{def:set_ops}
$\forall x,\; x \in \texttt{osetReplacement}(F, a) \leftrightarrow \exists\,
y \in a,\; F(y) = x$.
Proved via \texttt{Classical.allZFSetDefinable}: all Lean functions $F$
are treated as definable.  This introduces \texttt{Classical.choice}
(\emph{second source}; see Observation~C.1).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~III.8 --- Foundation]
\label{thm:T_III_8}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_8_Foundation}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
$\forall a \ne \emptyset,\; \exists\, x \in a,\; \forall y \in x,\; y \notin a$.
Proved via \texttt{WellFounded.has\_min} on \texttt{IsWellFounded.wf}
(\texttt{Classical.choice} is the \emph{third source}).
\emph{Fourth structural boundary}: Foundation holds unconditionally on
\texttt{OSet} because \texttt{ZFSet} is well-founded by construction.
In ZFA-mode it would fail; but ZFA-mode is absent (Observation~B.5).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~III.9 --- Choice]
\label{thm:T_III_9}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_9_Choice}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
If every member of $a$ is nonempty, then $\exists\, f : \mathrm{OSet}
\to \mathrm{OSet},\; \forall x \in a,\; f(x) \in x$.
Proved via \texttt{Classical.epsilon} (\texttt{Classical.choice}
directly, the \emph{fourth source}).  The preprint argues AC is a
theorem of the countable operational universe (via DC); this argument
is metatheoretic.
\end{theorem}

\lean{VR.Sets.isZFCmode}
\leanok

$\texttt{isZFCmode}(s) := \texttt{Acc}\,({\cdot} \in {\cdot})\,s$
--- accessibility of $s$ in the membership relation.  A set is in

\end{definition}

\label{thm:isZFCmode_all}
\lean{VR.Sets.isZFCmode_all}

well-founded because \texttt{PSet} is an inductive type.  The theorem
records that $\texttt{OSet} := \texttt{ZFSet}$ lies entirely within
ZFC-mode.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~IV.1 --- All nine ZFC axioms in ZFC-mode]
\label{thm:Theorem_IV_1}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_IV_1_ZFCAxioms}
\leanok
\uses{thm:Lemma_II_1, thm:Lemma_II_2, thm:T_III_3, thm:T_III_4,

The conjunction of all nine ZFC axioms (Extensionality, Empty, Foundation,

\subsection{ZFA boundary: Quine atom and AFA refuted in PSet}
\label{subsec:zfa_refuted}

\begin{definition}[ZFA-mode predicate and PSet]
\label{def:isZFAmode}
\lean{VR.Sets.isZFAmode, VR.Sets.isZFAmode_all}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
$\texttt{isZFAmode}(\ldots) := \texttt{True}$ on $\texttt{PSet}$: every
PSet element is declared to be in ZFA-mode (maximal universe).
$\texttt{isZFAmode\_all}$ follows by \texttt{trivial}.
\axiomprofile{[]} for both.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quine atom specification]
\label{def:quineAtomSpec}
\lean{VR.Sets.quineAtomSpec}
\leanok
$\texttt{quineAtomSpec} := \exists\, p : \texttt{PSet}.{0},\; p \in p$.
The existence of a self-membered PSet element (Quine atom $A = \{A\}$).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Quine atom is impossible in PSet]
\label{thm:quineAtom_impossible}
\lean{VR.Sets.quineAtom_impossible}
\leanok
\uses{def:quineAtomSpec}
$\lnot\texttt{quineAtomSpec}$.
Proved by \texttt{PSet.mem\_irrefl}: \texttt{PSet} is an inductive type,
so membership is irreflexive.  The Quine atom would require $p \in p$,
which contradicts \texttt{PSet.mem\_irrefl}.

\noindent\axiomprofile{[]} --- axiom-free proof.  PSet's well-founded
recursion principle is structural, not axiomatic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Anti-Foundation Axiom (classical)]
\label{def:AFA_Statement}
\lean{VR.Sets.AFA_Statement}
\leanok
$\texttt{AFA\_Statement}$: for every graph $(V, E)$ (any Lean type $V$,
any relation $E$) there exists a unique decoration $f : V \to
\texttt{PSet}$ such that for every vertex $v$,
$f(v) \equiv \{f(w) \mid (v,w) \in E\}$ (in the sense of
\texttt{PSet.Equiv}).
This is Aczel's (1988) graph-decoration AFA in full classical generality.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[AFA is refuted in PSet]
\label{thm:AFA_Refuted}
\lean{VR.Sets.AFA_Refuted}
\leanok
\uses{def:AFA_Statement, thm:quineAtom_impossible}
$\lnot\texttt{AFA\_Statement}$.
Proof: apply \texttt{AFA\_Statement} to the universal self-loop graph
($V = \texttt{Unit}$, $E\,\_\,\_ = \texttt{True}$); the decoration $f$
must satisfy $f() \equiv \{f()\}$, giving $f() \in f()$; this
contradicts \texttt{PSet.mem\_irrefl}.

\noindent\axiomprofile{[]} --- axiom-free proof.

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:AFA_strongest_boundary}
\leanok
This is the strongest boundary result in VR-Sets.  At the earlier
structural boundaries (Theorems~III.5, III.7, III.9), Lean's object
\emph{existed} as a classical entity wider than the operational version.
Here, the ZFA-mode universe \emph{provably does not exist} in mathlib's
type hierarchy.  Adding AFA as an axiom would be inconsistent with
\texttt{PSet.mem\_irrefl}.  The impossibility is constructively proved,
not merely metatheoretic.  See Observation~B.5.
\end{remark}
\end{theorem}

\section{Operational consequences}
\label{sec:sets_consequences}

\begin{remark}[$\mathcal{P}(\omega)$ is countable in the operational reading]
\label{rem:powerset_countable}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_5_Power}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_5}
The preprint (§\,III.5) asserts that $\mathcal{P}(\omega)$ in VR-Sets is
countable: each element is a describable functionality (a finite
algorithm over a finite alphabet), and the set of such algorithms is
countable.  Cantor's diagonal $D = \{n : n \notin S_n\}$ requires a
complete enumeration of describable subsets --- operationally
unattainable (equivalent to solving the Halting Problem).  $D$ does
not specify a describable functionality, hence $D$ is not a set in the
operational universe.  The diagonal is absent from the operational
$\mathcal{P}(\omega)$ not by prohibition but because its premise is
unrealisable.  Lean's \texttt{osetPower} contains all classical subsets;
the countability claim is metatheoretic.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Axiom of Choice as theorem]
\label{rem:AC_theorem}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_9_Choice}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_9}
The preprint (§\,III.9) derives AC from Dependent Choice plus countability
of the operational universe.  AC is thus a theorem, not an axiom; the
Banach--Tarski paradox is excluded not by weakening AC but because the
uncountable subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ on which it relies do not exist in
the operational ontology.  Lean records AC via \texttt{Classical.choice}
for all families; the countability argument is metatheoretic.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Replacement schema collapses to one theorem]
\label{rem:replacement_schema}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_7_Replacement}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_7}
In classical ZF, Replacement is an axiom schema (one axiom per formula).
In VR-Sets and in Lean/mathlib, a single theorem quantifies over all
functions $F$.  The schema collapses because Lean functions are
first-class objects: the meta-level quantification over formulas
disappears.  See Observation~D.1.
\end{remark}

\end{remark}

\section{Methodological observations (Part X)}
\label{sec:sets_method}

Seventeen observations from the formalisation, grouped thematically.
Observations A.1--D.2 are from the Part~X preprint draft; observations
E.1--E.6 are from source-file comments.

\subsection*{Group A: Quotient-base structure}

\begin{remark}[A.1 --- Bisimulation as definitional equality]
\label{rem:A1_bisim}
\lean{VR.Sets.OSet}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
The preprint's operational identity $\equiv$ (Definition~4, bisimulation)
is not a separate proof step but is definitionally absorbed into the
quotient construction: $a \equiv b$ is exactly $a = b : \texttt{OSet}
= \texttt{ZFSet} = \texttt{Quotient PSet.setoid}$.  This is more
economical than VR-Numbers, where each isomorphism required explicit
\texttt{forward}/\texttt{backward} round-trip proofs.  Here, the
bisimulation \emph{is} the quotient.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A.2 --- Duplicate suppression via quotient]
\label{rem:A2_dedup}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_4_Union}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_4}
The preprint (§\,II.2) says union reveals elements «without repetitions
up to $\equiv$».  In \texttt{ZFSet}, duplicate suppression requires no
separate predicate or decidability instance: the quotient automatically
identifies extensionally equivalent elements.  \texttt{ZFSet.sUnion}
and \texttt{ZFSet.powerset} return \texttt{ZFSet} values, so membership
is already modulo \texttt{PSet.Equiv}.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Group B: Five structural boundaries}

\begin{remark}[B.1 --- First boundary: power set wider than operational]
\label{rem:B1_power}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_5_Power}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_5}
Lean's \texttt{osetPower} contains all classical subsets.  The
preprint's $\mathcal{P}(A)$ contains only operationally describable
subsets.  For $A = \omega$, Lean gives an uncountable set; the
preprint gives a countable set.  The operational restriction is
metatheoretic.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[B.2 --- Second boundary: replacement wider than operational]
\label{rem:B2_repl}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_7_Replacement}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_7}
\texttt{osetReplacement} admits all Lean functions $F$.  The preprint
restricts to operationally definable functions.  \texttt{Classical.allZFSetDefinable}
records the gap: «all Lean functions are definable» is the non-operational
assumption.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[B.3 --- Third boundary: choice wider than countable]
\label{rem:B3_choice}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_9_Choice}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_9}
Lean proves AC for all families via \texttt{Classical.choice}.  The
preprint argues AC from countability: in a countable universe, a choice
function can be constructed algorithmically (DC suffices).  Lean cannot
express the countability restriction; the argument is metatheoretic.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[B.4 --- Fourth boundary: Foundation unconditional, opposite direction]
\label{rem:B4_found}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_8_Foundation}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_8}
On \texttt{OSet = ZFSet}, Foundation holds unconditionally (Lean is
\emph{narrower} than the preprint).  The preprint treats Foundation as
mode-dependent (fails in ZFA-mode).  In Lean, there is no ZFA-mode to
speak of; the type pre-commits to well-foundedness.  This is the
opposite direction from boundaries B.1--B.3.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[B.5 --- Fifth boundary: ZFA mode totally absent from mathlib]
\label{rem:B5_zfa}
\lean{VR.Sets.quineAtom_impossible, VR.Sets.AFA_Refuted}
\leanok
\uses{thm:quineAtom_impossible, thm:AFA_Refuted}
A systematic search of all of mathlib4 for \texttt{AFA},
\texttt{AntiFoundation}, \texttt{non-well-founded}, coinductive sets,
\texttt{NonWellFounded}, and \texttt{Quine} returned zero results.
\texttt{PSet} is an inductive (not coinductive) Lean type; membership
is well-founded by its elimination rule.  The Quine atom and AFA are
not merely hard to construct --- they are \emph{provably impossible}
in \texttt{PSet} with empty axiom profiles.  This boundary is
categorically stronger than B.1--B.4 and stronger than the
VR-Numbers §\,VIII.6 boundary: the ZFA-mode universe has no
representation at all in the mathlib type hierarchy.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Group C: Axiom-minimal patterns}

\begin{remark}[C.1 --- Four faces of \texttt{Classical.choice}]
\label{rem:C1_classical}
\lean{VR.Sets.Lemma_II_3_DepthMono, VR.Sets.Theorem_III_7_Replacement,
      VR.Sets.Theorem_III_8_Foundation, VR.Sets.Theorem_III_9_Choice}
\leanok
See Remark~\ref{rem:classical_four_sources} for details.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[C.2 --- Axiom profiles of conjectures reflect structural nature]
\label{rem:C2_profiles}
\lean{VR.Sets.Conjecture_IV_1_Statement, VR.Sets.Conjecture_IV_2_Statement}
\leanok
\uses{def:Conjecture_IV_1, def:Conjecture_IV_2}
\texttt{Conjecture\_IV\_1\_Statement}: \axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]}
(references \texttt{OSet = ZFSet}, a specific quotient type).
\texttt{Conjecture\_IV\_2\_Statement}: \axiomprofile{[]} (stated over
abstract $(U, \mathrm{mem})$, no reference to any specific mathlib type).
The difference is not incidental: Conjecture~IV.1 asks about a countable
submodel of an existing type; Conjecture~IV.2 asks about the existence of
a type \emph{outside} the current mathlib hierarchy.  The axiom profiles
witness this structural distinction at the level of dependency closures.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[C.3 --- \texttt{isZFCmode} has discriminating power only at PSet level]
\label{rem:C3_isZFCmode}
\lean{VR.Sets.isZFCmode, VR.Sets.isZFCmode_all}
\leanok
\uses{def:isZFCmode, thm:isZFCmode_all}
On \texttt{OSet = ZFSet}, \texttt{isZFCmode} is universally true;
the predicate is trivial here.  Its conceptual content becomes operative
only at the \texttt{PSet} level (Stage~10), where elements representing
the Quine atom would fail \texttt{Acc PSet.Mem} (the membership chain
$A \ni A \ni A \ni \ldots$ does not terminate).  The predicate is
defined at \texttt{OSet}-level as a syntactic template for that future
analysis.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[C.4 --- Operational depth is absent for cyclic sets in OSet]
\label{rem:C4_depth_cyclic}
\lean{VR.Sets.operationalDepth}
\leanok
\uses{def:operationalDepth}
Lemma~II.3 in the preprint has three cases: finite depth, infinite
non-cyclic depth (e.g.\ $\omega$), and cyclic/undefined depth (e.g.\
Quine atom).  In \texttt{OSet = ZFSet}, the third case is
\emph{structurally absent}: \texttt{PSet} is inductive, so cyclic
objects do not exist.  \texttt{operationalDepth} is well-defined
(total) on all \texttt{OSet} elements; the preprint's undefined case
simply does not arise in the type.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Group D: Methodological convergences}

\begin{remark}[D.1 --- Replacement: schema $\to$ single theorem]
\label{rem:D1_schema}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_III_7_Replacement}
\leanok
\uses{thm:T_III_7}
Classical ZF has Replacement as a schema (one axiom per formula).
VR-Sets has a single closure theorem over describable functions.
Lean/mathlib has a single theorem parametric in $F : \texttt{ZFSet}
\to \texttt{ZFSet}$.  Three systems, three different motivations
(classical meta-level quantification / operational describability /
type-theoretic first-class functions), same formal result: one theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[D.2 --- Three-tier formalisation result]
\label{rem:D2_three_tier}
\lean{VR.Sets.Theorem_IV_1_ZFCAxioms, VR.Sets.AFA_Refuted,
      VR.Sets.Conjecture_IV_2_Statement}
\leanok
VR-Numbers produced only positive results (theorems proved).  VR-Sets
produces a structurally richer three-tier outcome: (1)~16 proved
theorems (including \texttt{Theorem\_IV\_1\_ZFCAxioms},
\texttt{Theorem\_V\_2}); (2)~2 refuted claims
(\texttt{AFA\_Refuted}, \texttt{quineAtom\_impossible}), both axiom-free;
(3)~2 open formulations
(\texttt{Conjecture\_IV\_1\_Statement},
\texttt{Conjecture\_IV\_2\_Statement}).  The refuted claims are
\emph{structural boundary theorems}, not logical inconsistencies.
The open formulations are the first instances in the VR Lean cycle
where the system records a claim it cannot resolve.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[D.3 --- abbrev vs def for OSet: reducibility design]
\label{rem:D3_abbrev}
\lean{VR.Sets.OSet}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
\texttt{OSet} is declared as \texttt{abbrev} (\texttt{@[reducible]}),
not \texttt{def}.  This means all mathlib operations on \texttt{ZFSet}
apply directly to \texttt{OSet} without wrappers.  VR-Numbers used
\texttt{def} with explicit wrappers (\texttt{IntVRIntIso},
\texttt{iaddQ}, etc.) because VR-Numbers built its own arithmetic
independently.  VR-Sets relies on mathlib's \texttt{ZFSet}
infrastructure completely; \texttt{abbrev} reflects this dependency.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[D.4 --- Classical AFA vs operational AFA in Conjecture~IV.2]
\label{rem:D4_classical_afa}
\lean{VR.Sets.Conjecture_IV_2_Statement}
\leanok
\uses{def:Conjecture_IV_2}
\texttt{Conjecture\_IV\_2\_Statement} is stated with classical AFA
(all Lean types $V$, all relations $E$) --- strictly stronger than the
preprint's operational AFA (only describable graphs).  If the conjecture
is false, it does not refute the preprint's weaker form.  If true, it
implies the preprint's form.  Classical AFA is used because operational
AFA (like computability of Cauchy sequences in VR-Numbers) is not
Lean-expressible: the condition «describable graph» is metatheoretic.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[D.5 --- VR\_OSet\_iso covers ordinal structure only; arithmetic by composition]
\label{rem:D5_vr_oset_scope}
\lean{VR.Sets.VR_OSet_iso}
\leanok
\uses{def:VR_OSet_iso}
\texttt{VR\_OSet\_iso} has five fields: embed, preserve\_zero,
preserve\_succ, preserve\_mem, injective.  Arithmetic preservation
(\texttt{vadd}, \texttt{vmul}, \texttt{vpow} at OSet level) is
deliberately excluded.  It follows by composition:
$\mathrm{VRObj} \xrightarrow{\texttt{Theorem\_11\_VR\_PA}} \mathbb{N}
\xrightarrow{\texttt{embedVR} \circ O} \mathrm{OSet}$.
Re-proving at the OSet level would duplicate VR.lean without adding
mathematical content.  Contrast with \texttt{VR\_PA\_iso} (Chapter~\ref{chap:vr}):
that is a bijection; \texttt{VR\_OSet\_iso} is an embedding (OSet
contains vastly more than the image of VRObj).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[D.6 --- Closure principle is metatheoretic]
\label{rem:D6_closure}
\lean{VR.Sets.OSet}
\leanok
\uses{def:OSet}
The preprint's closure principle (§\,II.3): «if a functionality is
operationally describable, then there exists a set $A$ such that $A$
is that functionality» is \emph{not} expressible as a Lean predicate.
Individual closure theorems (Pairing, Union, \ldots) are proved
separately, each as a wrapper over a mathlib \texttt{ZFSet} lemma.
The general principle --- «every describable functionality is a set»
--- lies in the metatheory.
\end{remark}
