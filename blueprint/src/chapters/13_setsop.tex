\section{Position}
\label{sec:setsop_position}

This chapter is the \textbf{Brouwer rebuild of VR-Sets} (preprint v1.0.3): the
operational universe of sets, built \emph{from scratch} below the
\texttt{Classical.choice} floor, and the correction of the earlier \S VI.
Versions through 1.0.2 realised the ZFC axioms over mathlib's classical
\texttt{ZFSet} (Chapter~\ref{chap:sets}) and argued that the operational
universe is \emph{countable}, whence the Axiom of Choice is free and
Banach--Tarski cannot arise.  That reading is Bishop's, and it contradicts the
operational continuum (Chapter~\ref{chap:continuum}), which took the
\emph{Brouwer} path.  Here the universe of sets is rebuilt on that same path and
the cardinality core is corrected: the universe \emph{as becoming} is
\emph{non-enumerable} (choice-free), while only the \emph{describable} register
is countable.

The carrier deliberately avoids mathlib's coinductive machinery
(\texttt{PFunctor.M} of Chapter~\ref{chap:setszfa}), which is irreducibly
Tier-3 even at its destructor.  The Lean~4 formalisation lives self-contained
under \texttt{VRCycle/SetsOp/} (\texttt{Pointed}, \texttt{Builder},
\texttt{Closure}, \texttt{Omega}, \texttt{Extensionality}, \texttt{Congruence},
\texttt{Becoming}, \texttt{Describable}, \texttt{Schemas}, \texttt{Grounded},
\texttt{Power}).
After the 2026-07-12 tier pass (Iff-\texttt{rw} replaced by combinators)
every object is \axiomprofile{[]} --- the empty axiom list --- except the
describable-register encoding flagged in \S\ref{sec:setsop_cardinality}.

\section{The carrier: sets as revealing functionalities}
\label{sec:setsop_carrier}

An \textbf{operational set} is a pointed graph $(V, E, \mathit{pt})$: $E\,a\,b$
reads ``querying the functionality at $b$ reveals $a$ as a member'';
$\mathit{pt}$ is the set itself; $x.\mathrm{child}\,a := (V,E,a)$ is the member
sitting at $a$.  Identity is supplied operationally and \textbf{no quotient is
taken}.

\begin{definition}[Operational set and witnessed identity]
\label{def:setsop_opset}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.Equiv, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.Mem}
\leanok
\texttt{OpSet} is the pointed graph; $x \approx y$ (\texttt{OpSet.Equiv}) holds
when a \emph{bisimulation} relates their points; $z \in x$ (\texttt{OpSet.Mem})
when some member-vertex of $x$ is $\approx$ to $z$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[$\approx$ is an equivalence]
\label{thm:setsop_equiv}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.Equiv.refl, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.Equiv.symm, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.Equiv.trans}
\leanok
\texttt{Equiv.refl/symm/trans} --- each \axiomprofile{[]} (axiom-free).
\end{theorem}

Turning ``bisimilar'' into a type-level equality (forming the quotient) requires
Skolemising the existential matches of the bisimulation into functions; that is
exactly VR's $T\!\to\!O$ asymmetry (Chapter~\ref{chap:forms}), and it is where
mathlib's coinductive layer invokes \texttt{Classical.choice}.  We therefore keep
identity as a relation carried with its witness --- a \emph{performed}
bisimulation, ``doing not being'' made into the construction --- which is why the
development stays below the floor.

\begin{theorem}[Strong extensionality]
\label{thm:setsop_ext}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.ext, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.equiv_iff_same_mem, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_congr}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_equiv}
Membership respects identity (\texttt{mem\_congr}, \axiomprofile{[]}), and
$x \approx y \iff \forall z,\ (z\in x \leftrightarrow z\in y)$
(\texttt{equiv\_iff\_same\_mem}; backward direction \texttt{ext}).  Both
\axiomprofile{[]}.  The backward direction is the coinductive content (a
bisimulation is \emph{built} from the member-matching); it is AFA-style strong
extensionality, kept choice-free precisely because identity was never
quotiented.  This licenses $\approx$ as a faithful equality of sets.
\end{theorem}

\section{Operations}
\label{sec:setsop_ops}

All operations instantiate one constructor: $\mathrm{sup}\,c$, the set whose
members are exactly the family $c$.

\begin{theorem}[Family supremum and its membership law]
\label{thm:setsop_sup}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.sup, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_sup}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_ext}
$z \in \mathrm{sup}\,c \iff \exists i,\ z \approx c\,i$ (\texttt{mem\_sup},
\axiomprofile{[]}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Empty, singleton, pair, union, successor]
\label{thm:setsop_basic}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.not_mem_emptySup, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_pair, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_union, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_succ}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_sup}
From \texttt{mem\_sup}: $\varnothing$ (\texttt{emptySup}, $z\notin\varnothing$),
singleton ($z\in\{a\}\iff z\approx a$), pair
($z\in\{a,b\}\iff z\approx a\vee z\approx b$), union
($z\in\bigcup x\iff\exists y,\ y\in x\wedge z\in y$), and successor
$\mathrm{succ}\,a := a\cup\{a\}$ ($z\in\mathrm{succ}\,a\iff z\in a\vee z\approx a$).
Each \axiomprofile{[]}.  The operations respect
$\approx$ (\texttt{*\_congr}, via Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_ext}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Infinity]
\label{thm:setsop_omega}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_omega, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.omega_succ_closed}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_basic}
The von Neumann naturals $\mathrm{vn}$ and $\omega := \mathrm{sup}\,\mathrm{vn}$:
the members of $\omega$ are exactly the $\mathrm{vn}\,n$ up to $\approx$;
$\varnothing\in\omega$; and $\omega$ is closed under successor for any member
(\texttt{omega\_succ\_closed}).  All \axiomprofile{[]}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Separation and Replacement]
\label{thm:setsop_schemas}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_sep, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_repl}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_sup}
For a species $p$ respecting $\approx$,
$z\in\{z\in x\mid p\,z\}\iff z\in x\wedge p\,z$ (\texttt{mem\_sep}).  For a rule
$F$ respecting $\approx$, $w\in\{F\,z\mid z\in x\}\iff\exists z,\ z\in x\wedge
w\approx F\,z$ (\texttt{mem\_repl}).  No decidability is needed; both
\axiomprofile{[]}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Power set --- choice-free but impredicative]
\label{thm:setsop_power}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.powerset, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_powerset}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_ext, thm:setsop_sup}
$\wp\,x := \mathrm{sup}$ over the vertex-predicates $s : x.V \to \mathrm{Prop}$
(\texttt{subsetOf}\,$x\,s$ keeps the member-vertices in $s$), with
$z \in \wp\,x \iff z \subseteq x$ (\texttt{mem\_powerset}); the backward
direction selects $s\,a :=$ ``$a$ is a member-vertex whose member lies in $z$''
and closes by strong extensionality (Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_ext}).
\axiomprofile{[]} --- axiom-free.  But see
Finding~\ref{find:setsop_impredicative}: the construction is below the choice
floor yet \emph{impredicative}.  With it, \texttt{OpSet} verifies every ZF$^-$
axiom (Extensionality, $\varnothing$, Pairing, Union, Power, Separation,
Replacement, Infinity) choice-free; Foundation is the predicate
\texttt{IsGrounded} (\S\ref{sec:setsop_grounded}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{finding}[The obstruction to power set is impredicativity, not choice]
\label{find:setsop_impredicative}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_powerset}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_power, thm:setsop_nonenum}
A \emph{completed} power set $\wp\,x$ is constructible \emph{choice-free}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_power}); in particular $\wp\,\omega$ exists as a set with
$z\in\wp\,\omega\iff z\subseteq\omega$.  What this costs is not
\texttt{Classical.choice} --- the axiom audit reports the empty list --- but \emph{impredicativity}: the index $x.V\to\mathrm{Prop}$ is the full
power of the representative's vertex type, a move legitimate in Lean's
impredicative \texttt{Prop} but outside a predicative (Brouwer / Martin-L\"of)
discipline, and one that \texttt{\#print axioms} cannot detect.  The operational
register's relation to power set is therefore sharper than ``absent'': the
\emph{predicative} content of $\wp\,\omega$ is the becoming
(Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_nonenum}, the diagonal), while the \emph{impredicative
completed} $\wp\,\omega$ also exists, choice-free, at the cost named here ---
isolating the obstruction as impredicativity, not choice.
\end{finding}

\section{Foundation is a predicate, not a choice}
\label{sec:setsop_grounded}

Classical set theory must decide, at the axiom level, whether $\in$-descent
always terminates (Foundation) or may cycle (Aczel's AFA,
Chapter~\ref{chap:setszfa}).  VR is not forced to: well-foundedness is a
\emph{property of a set's graph}, observed not postulated.

\begin{theorem}[Groundedness is $\approx$-invariant and hereditary]
\label{thm:setsop_grounded}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.isGrounded_iff, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.mem_grounded, VRCycle.SetsOp.acc_bisim}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_ext}
\texttt{IsGrounded}\,$x := \mathrm{Acc}\,(E)\,x.\mathit{pt}$.  Accessibility
transports along a bisimulation (\texttt{acc\_bisim}), so
$x\approx y\Rightarrow(\,x$ grounded $\iff y$ grounded$)$
(\texttt{isGrounded\_iff}); and a member of a grounded set is grounded
(\texttt{mem\_grounded}).  All \axiomprofile{[]}.  Hence ``well-founded /
ZFC-mode'' is a well-defined hereditary property of \emph{sets}, not of graphs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[ZFC and ZFA as fragments, not a choice]
\label{thm:setsop_fragments}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.quine_self_mem, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.quine_not_grounded, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.vn_not_self_mem}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_grounded}
The ZFC fragment is the grounded sets ($\varnothing$ grounded; the von Neumann
naturals are $\in$-acyclic, \texttt{vn\_not\_self\_mem}, by strong induction).
The Quine atom $A=\{A\}$ (\texttt{quine}) is a genuine operational set with
$A\in A$ (\texttt{quine\_self\_mem}) and is \emph{not} grounded
(\texttt{quine\_not\_grounded}) --- no anti-foundation axiom invoked, the graph
simply has a cycle.  VR thus holds ZFC and ZFA natively, as fragments of one
universe under a uniform (bisimulation) identity, and faces neither Foundation
nor AFA as a postulate.  All \axiomprofile{[]}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Anti-Foundation as an axiom-free theorem]
\label{thm:setsop_afa}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.afa, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.decorate_isDecoration, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.decoration_unique}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_fragments, thm:setsop_ext}
Because membership is the edge relation and a member is the same graph re-pointed
(\texttt{child}), nesting --- including self-membership --- is native, and Aczel's
decoration is almost definitional.  Every graph $(V,E)$ has a decoration
$\texttt{decorate}\,E\,v:=(V,E,v)$ (\texttt{decorate\_isDecoration}, by
\texttt{rfl} --- membership \emph{is} the decoration condition), unique up to
$\approx$ (\texttt{decoration\_unique}; for each $v$ the choice-free
\emph{exhibited} bisimulation $R_v\,a\,w := (d\,v).\texttt{child}\,a \approx
d\,w$).  \axiomprofile{[]} --- \textbf{fully axiom-free}.

\emph{Cost of the quotient (bridge to Chapter~\ref{chap:setszfa}).}  VR-Sets-ZFA
obtains the same content on mathlib's M-types by \emph{quotienting}; there AFA is
\axiomprofile{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} --- forming the quotient
Skolemises the bisimulation's witnesses (transit asymmetry,
Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_ext} ff.) and uniqueness selects representatives by
\texttt{Classical.choice} through the choice-pulling \texttt{PFunctor.M.dest}.
The relational identity is axiom-free precisely where its quotient sits at the
full ceiling.  We read the two works together as
$\mathrm{OSetZFA}\simeq\texttt{OpSet}/\!\approx$ (this version the choice-free
floor of which VR-Sets-ZFA is the quotient) --- a \emph{prose-level reading, not
yet machine-checked} (in the modality of Conjecture~IV.2); its formalisation (a
\texttt{Bridge} file: \texttt{M.corec} unfolding, descent through the quotient,
bijectivity on $\approx$-classes) is a candidate for VR-Sets-ZFA~v1.1.
\end{theorem}

\section{Cardinality, the diagonal, and choice --- the corrected core}
\label{sec:setsop_cardinality}

\begin{theorem}[The operational universe is non-enumerable]
\label{thm:setsop_nonenum}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.universe_not_enumerable, VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.vn_inj}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_fragments, thm:setsop_ext}
No $\mathbb{N}$-indexed enumeration is surjective up to $\approx$:
$\lnot\,\exists e:\mathbb{N}\to\texttt{OpSet},\ \forall x,\ \exists n,\ e\,n\approx x$
(\texttt{universe\_not\_enumerable}, \axiomprofile{[]} --- axiom-free).  The proof is a
self-contained Cantor diagonal \emph{inside} the universe: the diagonal set
$D := \{\mathrm{vn}\,n \mid \mathrm{vn}\,n\notin e\,n\}$ (a \texttt{sup} over a
subtype, no decidability) satisfies, by the distinctness of the naturals
(\texttt{vn\_inj}, \axiomprofile{[]}),
$\mathrm{vn}\,k\in D\iff\mathrm{vn}\,k\notin e\,k$; were $e$ surjective, some
$e\,m\approx D$ forces $\mathrm{vn}\,m\in e\,m\iff\mathrm{vn}\,m\notin e\,m$.
This is the Brouwerian content: the diagonal \emph{works}, cutting done from
becoming; the universe is genuinely uncountable, choice-free.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The describable register is countable]
\label{thm:setsop_describable}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.describable_countable}
\leanok
A finite description syntax \texttt{Desc} ($\varnothing$, pair, union, $\omega$)
with interpretation \texttt{eval} gives ``describable'' sets; an \emph{exhibited}
$\mathbb{N}$-enumeration (\texttt{descEnum}) reaches every describable set up to
$\approx$ (\texttt{describable\_countable}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{finding}[The one borrowed \texttt{Classical.choice}]
\label{find:setsop_choice}
\lean{VRCycle.SetsOp.OpSet.describable_countable}
\leanok
\uses{thm:setsop_describable}
Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_describable} is the only Tier-3 object, depending on
\texttt{Classical.choice}, and the dependence is \emph{borrowed}: it enters
solely through mathlib's pairing inversion (\texttt{Nat.unpair\_pair} /
\texttt{Nat.pair\_eq\_pair} are both \axiomprofile{[propext, Classical.choice,
Quot.sound]}; \texttt{Nat.pair} itself is \axiomprofile{[]}).  The fact is
constructively true; mathlib proves it with incidental choice.  The countability
of a register of \emph{descriptions} is in any case formal-register
meta-accounting, where classical reasoning is admitted with its cost made
visible.  Kept and documented (decision A).
\end{finding}

Theorems~\ref{thm:setsop_nonenum} and~\ref{thm:setsop_describable} together are
the corrected \S VI: \textbf{the describable (done) register is countable, the
universe (becoming) is not.}  Consequently the Axiom of Choice is \emph{not} an
operational theorem.  Dependent choice is operational
(Theorem~\ref{thm:continuum_dc}); full AC is a formal-register label with no
operational correlate.  Banach--Tarski is a $T\!\to\!T$ phenomenon of the
full-AC context --- a completed uncountable totality plus selection by full AC,
neither with an operational correlate --- which by conservativity
(Chapter~\ref{chap:forms}) never transits to the operational register: its
absence operationally is a conservativity phenomenon, not a cardinality accident.

\section{Honest scope}
\label{sec:setsop_scope}

\textbf{Below the floor by construction.}  ``Choice-free'' is a statement about
audited axiom dependencies, exhibited build-time; Lean's ambient logic remains
classical.  \textbf{Two reals.}  The operational reals (becoming,
Chapter~\ref{chap:continuum}) and $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{VR}}\cong\mathbb{R}$
(uncountable, Chapter~\ref{chap:numbers}) are distinct objects; this chapter
commits the set universe to the Brouwer reading.  \textbf{Power set is
impredicative, not choice-laden.}  A completed power set $\wp\,x$ \emph{is}
constructible choice-free (Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_power}), but only
impredicatively (Finding~\ref{find:setsop_impredicative}); the \emph{predicative}
content of $\wp(\omega)$ is the becoming, Theorem~\ref{thm:setsop_nonenum}.
\textbf{The earlier version stands} as the classical-surrogate presentation
(Chapter~\ref{chap:sets}); only its \S VI cardinality reading is superseded here.
