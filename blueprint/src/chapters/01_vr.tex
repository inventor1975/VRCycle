\chapter{VR Formal System}
\label{chap:vr}

\section{Position}

The VR formal system constructs arithmetic from \textbf{two} primitive
\emph{operations}---the nullary base~$\emptyset$ and the succession
operator~$t$.  (Versions through v1.1.0 listed a third, a propositional
implication~$\to$ on a two-valued type; v1.1.1 removes it---it generated
only a finite truth-function algebra on a $2$-element type, used by no VR
theorem.  That is arithmetic on a finite type, not logic in any
inferential sense.  The logic VR reasons \emph{with}---$\forall$, $\to$,
$\leftrightarrow$, induction, Leibnizian equality---is metatheoretic,
borrowed from the ambient framework, not generated by VR; see
Remark~\ref{rem:iff_two_senses}.)  It posits \emph{no axioms of its own}:
membership $\in$ is \emph{defined} by recursion on~$t$; succession~A3 is a
\emph{theorem}; induction~A4 is the \emph{recursor} of the inductive
term-algebra.  ``Axiom-free'' is meant relative to the type-theoretic
framework: VR adds no postulate beyond the free term-algebra on
$\{\emptyset, t\}$ and its recursor, matching the $[\,]$ axiom profile of
the Lean~4 companion.  The main result of Part~I is Theorem~11: a
structural isomorphism $\mathbb{N} \simeq \mathrm{VRObj}$ preserving zero,
successor, addition, multiplication, and exponentiation---VR and
first-order Peano arithmetic are arithmetically equivalent.  All public
objects are axiom-free.

The formalisation yields six methodological observations that sharpen
the preprint's claims.

\begin{remark}[A4 as theorem]
\label{rem:A4_theorem}
\lean{VR.A4_induction}
\leanok
Axiom~A4 (induction) is not postulated in Lean: it is a provable theorem
(\texttt{VR.A4\_induction}) derived automatically from the recursor of the
inductive type \texttt{VRObj}.  The postulation becomes superfluous once
the inductive type is declared.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[P1 and P2 absorbed by typing]
\label{rem:P1P2}
\lean{VR.O_zero, VR.O_succ}
\leanok
Peano axioms P1 ($O_0$ exists as an object) and P2 ($t(O_n)$ exists for
every $O_n$) require existential proofs in an untyped first-order
setting.  In Lean they become typing judgements: \texttt{VR.O\_zero :
O~0 = VRObj.base} and \texttt{VRObj.succ : VRObj → VRObj}.  They have
been absorbed into the type system; no separate theorems are needed.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Theorem~11 as structural isomorphism]
\label{rem:T11_iso}
\lean{VR.Theorem_11_VR_PA}
\leanok
The preprint states Theorem~11 as a metatheoretic equivalence of theorem
sets.  The Lean object \texttt{VR.Theorem\_11\_VR\_PA} is a concrete
term of type \texttt{VR\_PA\_iso}: a nine-field structure recording
forward and backward maps, bijectivity, and preservation of zero,
successor, addition, multiplication, and exponentiation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[T1--T4 not needed for equivalence]
\label{rem:T1T4_not_needed}
\lean{VR.O_add, VR.O_mul, VR.O_pow}
\leanok
The operation isomorphisms \texttt{VR.O\_add}, \texttt{VR.O\_mul}, and
\texttt{VR.O\_pow} are proved by direct structural induction on the Lean
natural number argument, without invoking~T1, T2, T3, or~T4.  The
arithmetic properties of VR operations are thus not prerequisites for
the equivalence theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The logic is metatheoretic, not VR-generated]
\label{rem:iff_two_senses}
\lean{VR.vrEq}
\leanok
VR generates no logic of its own.  Its reasoning---the $\forall$ and
$\leftrightarrow$ of Leibnizian equality (\texttt{vrEq}), the induction
of~A4, the connectives of every theorem statement---uses the ambient
(metatheoretic) logic of the type-theoretic framework.  The Lean
definition of \texttt{vrEq} as $\forall p,\ p(x) \leftrightarrow p(y)$
uses Lean's \texttt{Iff}; there is no VR-internal propositional operator.
Version~1.1.1 removed the former \texttt{VRBool}/\texttt{impl} layer---a
finite truth-function algebra that was logic only in name.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Acyclicity of $\in$ without external measure]
\label{rem:acyclicity_no_measure}
\lean{VR.not_mem_self}
\leanok
Irreflexivity of membership (\texttt{not\_mem\_self}) is proved by two
structural auxiliary lemmas (\texttt{mem\_succ\_left} and
\texttt{mem\_asymm}, both private) without introducing a depth
function~$\mathrm{depth}: \mathrm{VRObj} \to \mathbb{N}$.  Acyclicity
of~$\in$ is thus established by the internal means of the inductive
type alone.
\end{remark}

\section{Primitives}
\label{sec:vr_primitives}

\begin{definition}[Objects of the VR system]
\label{def:VRObj}
\lean{VR.VRObj}
\leanok
The type \texttt{VRObj} is the inductive type with two constructors:
\[
  \texttt{base} : \mathrm{VRObj}, \qquad
  \texttt{succ} : \mathrm{VRObj} \to \mathrm{VRObj}.
\]
It represents the domain generated from the base operation $\emptyset$
by iterated application of~$t$.  Lean's induction principle for
\texttt{VRObj} is exactly Axiom~A4.
\end{definition}

\section{The named principles A3, A4 (theorem and recursor)}
\label{sec:vr_axioms}

Earlier versions stated four named principles A1--A4 as axioms.  A1 and~A2
governed the removed propositional layer; v1.1.1 drops them.  What remains
is purely operational: A3 (succession) is a \emph{theorem}, A4 (induction)
is the \emph{recursor} of the inductive term-algebra.  VR posits no axioms
of its own.

\begin{definition}[Membership and subset]
\label{def:mem}
\lean{VR.mem, VR.subset}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj}
Membership $\texttt{mem} : \mathrm{VRObj} \to \mathrm{VRObj} \to
\mathrm{Prop}$ is defined by structural recursion: $x \in
\texttt{base}$ is false; $x \in \texttt{succ}\,y$ holds iff $x = y$
or $x \in y$.  Subset is $x \subseteq y \iff \forall z,\, z \in x \to z \in y$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[A3 --- Succession]
\label{thm:A3}
\lean{VR.A3_mem_self, VR.A3_subset_succ}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj, def:mem}
For every $x : \mathrm{VRObj}$: (i)~$x \in t(x)$ (\texttt{A3\_mem\_self}),
proved as \texttt{Or.inl rfl}; (ii)~$x \subseteq t(x)$
(\texttt{A3\_subset\_succ}), proved as \texttt{Or.inr}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A4 --- Induction]
\label{thm:A4}
\lean{VR.A4_induction, VR.A4_exhaustion}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj}
\texttt{A4\_induction}: any property holding at \texttt{base} and
preserved by \texttt{succ} holds for all \texttt{VRObj}; proved by the
\texttt{induction} tactic from the recursor of \texttt{VRObj}.
\texttt{A4\_exhaustion}: every \texttt{VRObj} is either \texttt{base}
or of the form \texttt{succ}~$y$; proved by \texttt{cases}.  See
Remark~\ref{rem:A4_theorem}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Leibnizian equality}
\label{sec:vr_leibniz}

\begin{definition}[Leibnizian equality and distinctness]
\label{def:vrEq}
\lean{VR.vrEq, VR.vrNe}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj}
$\texttt{vrEq}\,x\,y := \forall (p : \mathrm{VRObj} \to \mathrm{Prop}),\;
p\,x \leftrightarrow p\,y$, quantified over all Lean predicates.
Distinctness: $\texttt{vrNe}\,x\,y := \lnot\,\texttt{vrEq}\,x\,y$.
The $\leftrightarrow$ here is Lean's propositional \texttt{Iff}
(metatheoretic, the ambient logic); see Remark~\ref{rem:iff_two_senses}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lean equality implies Leibnizian equality]
\label{thm:Eq_to_vrEq}
\lean{VR.Eq_to_vrEq}
\leanok
\uses{def:vrEq}
If $x = y$ (Lean equality), then $\texttt{vrEq}\,x\,y$.  Proved by
\texttt{subst} followed by \texttt{Iff.rfl}.
\end{theorem}

%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Von Neumann ordinals}
\label{sec:vr_ordinals}

\begin{definition}[Von Neumann ordinals]
\label{def:O}
\lean{VR.O}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj}
The function $O : \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{VRObj}$ is defined by $O(0) :=
\texttt{base}$ and $O(n+1) := \texttt{succ}(O(n))$.  Lean's
\texttt{Nat} serves as an external index; the VR objects are the image
of~$O$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Concrete values]
\label{thm:O_concrete}
\lean{VR.O_one, VR.O_two, VR.O_three}
\leanok
\uses{def:O}
$O(1) = \texttt{succ}\,\texttt{base}$, $O(2) = \texttt{succ}(\texttt{succ}\,\texttt{base})$,
$O(3) = \texttt{succ}(\texttt{succ}(\texttt{succ}\,\texttt{base}))$; all by \texttt{rfl}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$O_k \in O_n$ for $k < n$]
\label{thm:O_mem_lt}
\lean{VR.O_mem_lt}
\leanok
\uses{def:O, def:mem, thm:A3}
For every $k < n$, $O(k) \in O(n)$.  Proved by induction on the proof
of $k < n$ using the constructors of \texttt{Nat.le}: the base case is
\texttt{A3\_mem\_self}, and the step embeds via \texttt{Or.inr}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Arithmetic}
\label{sec:vr_arith}

\begin{definition}[Addition, multiplication, exponentiation]
\label{def:vadd}
\lean{VR.vadd}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj}
$\texttt{vadd}(a, \texttt{base}) := a$;\quad
$\texttt{vadd}(a, \texttt{succ}\,b) := \texttt{succ}(\texttt{vadd}(a, b))$.
Structural recursion on the second argument.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:vmul}
\lean{VR.vmul}
\leanok
\uses{def:vadd}
$\texttt{vmul}(a, \texttt{base}) := \texttt{base}$;\quad
$\texttt{vmul}(a, \texttt{succ}\,b) := \texttt{vadd}(\texttt{vmul}(a,b),\,a)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:vpow}
\lean{VR.vpow}
\leanok
\uses{def:vmul}
$\texttt{vpow}(a, \texttt{base}) := \texttt{succ}\,\texttt{base}$ (i.e.~$O(1)$);\quad
$\texttt{vpow}(a, \texttt{succ}\,b) := \texttt{vmul}(\texttt{vpow}(a,b),\,a)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Left neutral and left-successor for addition]
\label{lem:vadd_aux}
\lean{VR.vadd_zero_left, VR.vadd_succ_left}
\leanok
\uses{def:vadd}
$\texttt{vadd}(\texttt{base}, b) = b$ (\texttt{vadd\_zero\_left}) and
$\texttt{vadd}(\texttt{succ}\,a, b) = \texttt{succ}(\texttt{vadd}(a, b))$
(\texttt{vadd\_succ\_left}); both proved by induction on~$b$.  Used in
the proof of~T1.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[T1 --- Commutativity of addition]
\label{thm:T1}
\lean{VR.T1_vadd_comm}
\leanok
\uses{def:vadd, lem:vadd_aux}
$\texttt{vadd}\,a\,b = \texttt{vadd}\,b\,a$ for all $a, b :
\mathrm{VRObj}$.  By induction on~$b$: the base case uses
\texttt{vadd\_zero\_left}; the step uses \texttt{vadd\_succ\_left} and
the inductive hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[T2 --- Associativity of addition]
\label{thm:T2}
\lean{VR.T2_vadd_assoc}
\leanok
\uses{def:vadd}
$(\texttt{vadd}\,a\,b)\,c = \texttt{vadd}\,a\,(\texttt{vadd}\,b\,c)$
for all $a, b, c$.  By direct induction on~$c$; no auxiliary lemmas
needed beyond the definition of \texttt{vadd}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[T3 --- Distributivity]
\label{thm:T3}
\lean{VR.T3_vmul_distrib}
\leanok
\uses{def:vmul, def:vadd, thm:T2}
$\texttt{vmul}\,a\,(\texttt{vadd}\,b\,c) = \texttt{vadd}\,(\texttt{vmul}\,a\,b)\,(\texttt{vmul}\,a\,c)$
for all $a, b, c$.  By induction on~$c$; associativity of addition (T2)
is the key step to redistribute the $a$-summand at the inductive step.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[T4 --- $O_1 + O_1 = O_2$]
\label{thm:T4}
\lean{VR.T4_one_plus_one}
\leanok
\uses{def:vadd, def:O}
$\texttt{vadd}(O(1),\,O(1)) = O(2)$; proved by \texttt{rfl} via two
definitional reductions of \texttt{vadd}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Peano correspondence}
\label{sec:vr_peano}

\begin{theorem}[Correspondence: $0 \mapsto O_0$, $\mathrm{S} \mapsto t$]
\label{thm:peano_corr}
\lean{VR.O_zero, VR.O_succ}
\leanok
\uses{def:O}
$\texttt{O\_zero}: O(0) = \texttt{base}$ and $\texttt{O\_succ}: O(n+1) =
\texttt{succ}(O(n))$; both by \texttt{rfl}.  These name the two defining
equations of~$O$ explicitly to record the Peano correspondence $0
\mapsto O_0$, $\mathrm{Nat.succ} \mapsto \texttt{VRObj.succ}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[P1 and P2]
\label{rem:P1P2_detail}
\leanok
\uses{thm:peano_corr}
Peano axioms P1 ($O_0$ is an object) and P2 ($t(O_n)$ exists for every
$O_n$) require no separate proofs.  P1 is the typing judgement $O(0) :
\mathrm{VRObj}$; P2 is the total function $\texttt{succ} :
\mathrm{VRObj} \to \mathrm{VRObj}$.  They have been absorbed into the
type system.  This is a methodological observation, not a defect.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[P3 --- Successor is not zero]
\label{thm:P3}
\lean{VR.P3_succ_ne_zero}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj}
$\texttt{succ}\,x \ne \texttt{base}$ for every $x : \mathrm{VRObj}$.
Proved via \texttt{VRObj.noConfusion}: \texttt{base} and \texttt{succ}
are distinct constructors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[P4 --- Injectivity of successor]
\label{thm:P4}
\lean{VR.P4_succ_inj_leibniz, VR.P4_succ_inj}
\leanok
\uses{def:vrEq}
\texttt{P4\_succ\_inj\_leibniz}: if $\texttt{vrEq}(\texttt{succ}\,x,
\texttt{succ}\,y)$ then $\texttt{vrEq}(x, y)$; proved via the predicate
$q\,z := $ if $z = \texttt{base}$ then $\top$ else $p(\text{predecessor of }z)$.
\texttt{P4\_succ\_inj}: if $\texttt{succ}\,x = \texttt{succ}\,y$ (Lean
equality) then $x = y$; proved by projecting the constructor argument.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[P5 --- Induction]
\label{thm:P5}
\lean{VR.P5_induction}
\leanok
\uses{thm:A4}
The Peano induction axiom P5 coincides with A4 (\texttt{A4\_induction}).
\texttt{P5\_induction} is a named alias, establishing the explicit
correspondence without new proof.
\end{theorem}

\section{Acyclicity of membership}
\label{sec:vr_acyclicity}

\begin{theorem}[Irreflexivity of membership]
\label{thm:not_mem_self}
\lean{VR.not_mem_self}
\leanok
\uses{def:mem}
No object contains itself: $\lnot(x \in x)$ for all $x :
\mathrm{VRObj}$.  Derived from the private antisymmetry lemma
\texttt{mem\_asymm} (proved by induction on~$y$ using the private
\texttt{mem\_succ\_left}); the irreflexivity follows immediately.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$t(x) \ne x$]
\label{thm:succ_ne_self}
\lean{VR.succ_ne_self}
\leanok
\uses{def:vrEq, thm:A3, thm:not_mem_self}
$\texttt{vrNe}(\texttt{succ}\,x,\,x)$ for every $x$.  If $\texttt{vrEq}(\texttt{succ}\,x,\,x)$ held,
applying it to the predicate $p := \texttt{mem}\,x$ would give $x \in
\texttt{succ}\,x \leftrightarrow x \in x$.  The left side is true by A3;
the right side is false by \texttt{not\_mem\_self}.  See
Remark~\ref{rem:acyclicity_no_measure}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Main theorem --- VR $\simeq$ PA}
\label{sec:vr_T11}

\begin{definition}[Inverse translator]
\label{def:O_inv}
\lean{VR.O_inv}
\leanok
\uses{def:VRObj}
$O^{-1} : \mathrm{VRObj} \to \mathbb{N}$ by $O^{-1}(\texttt{base}) := 0$,
$O^{-1}(\texttt{succ}\,x) := O^{-1}(x) + 1$.  This is the Gödel
encoding of the preprint: $\lceil\emptyset\rceil := 0$,
$\lceil t(x)\rceil := \lceil x\rceil + 1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bijectivity of $O$]
\label{thm:O_bij}
\lean{VR.O_left_inv, VR.O_right_inv}
\leanok
\uses{def:O, def:O_inv}
Left inverse: $O^{-1}(O(n)) = n$ for all $n : \mathbb{N}$ (induction on~$n$).
Right inverse: $O(O^{-1}(x)) = x$ for all $x : \mathrm{VRObj}$
(induction on~$x$).  Together these establish that $O$ is a bijection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Operation isomorphisms]
\label{thm:O_ops}
\lean{VR.O_add, VR.O_mul, VR.O_pow}
\leanok
\uses{def:O, def:vadd, def:vmul, def:vpow}
$O(m + n) = \texttt{vadd}(O(m), O(n))$;
$O(m \cdot n) = \texttt{vmul}(O(m), O(n))$;
$O(m^n) = \texttt{vpow}(O(m), O(n))$;
all proved by structural induction on the second argument.
Note: T1--T4 are not invoked (see Remark~\ref{rem:T1T4_not_needed}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Isomorphism type]
\label{def:VR_PA_iso}
\lean{VR.VR_PA_iso}
\leanok
\uses{def:O, def:O_inv, def:vadd, def:vmul, def:vpow}
The structure \texttt{VR\_PA\_iso} has nine fields:
\texttt{forward}, \texttt{backward} (the maps), \texttt{left\_inv},
\texttt{right\_inv} (bijectivity), \texttt{preserve\_zero},
\texttt{preserve\_succ} (structural), and \texttt{preserve\_add},
\texttt{preserve\_mul}, \texttt{preserve\_pow} (arithmetic).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~11 --- VR $\simeq$ PA]
\label{thm:T11}
\lean{VR.Theorem_11_VR_PA}
\leanok
\uses{def:VR_PA_iso, def:O, def:O_inv, thm:O_bij, thm:O_ops,
      thm:peano_corr, def:vadd, def:vmul, def:vpow}
The term \texttt{Theorem\_11\_VR\_PA : VR\_PA\_iso} is constructed with
\texttt{forward := O}, \texttt{backward := O\_inv}, and the nine fields
filled by the previously proved theorems.  The preprint's metatheoretic
equivalence of theorem sets follows: any statement provable on
$\mathbb{N}$ transfers to $\mathrm{VRObj}$ and vice versa via
\texttt{forward}/\texttt{backward}.  This is the central result of the
chapter.
\end{theorem}

\section{Axiom profile}
\label{sec:vr_axiom_profile}

\axiomprofile{[]}

All public objects of the VR formal system are axiom-free.  No
\texttt{propext}, no \texttt{Classical.choice}, no \texttt{Quot.sound}.
This is the cleanest axiom profile in the VR Cycle: every result follows
from the inductive type \texttt{VRObj} and structural recursion alone.
No mathlib lemmas about Peano arithmetic are used; the only standard
library contribution is \texttt{Init.Data.Nat} at the definitional level
(the type \texttt{Nat} as external index).
