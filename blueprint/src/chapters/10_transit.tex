\section{Position}
\label{sec:transit_position}

VR-Transit is the tenth work of the cycle.  It extends VR-Apparatus
(Chapter~\ref{chap:apparatus}) from \emph{``transit is well-behaved''}
(closed under composition and identity; \texttt{Factorisable} as the canonical
Mode~B witness) to two further statements: that transit is \emph{conservative}
over axioms, and that the leverage it provides takes the form of a \emph{bounded
library of witnesses}.

\textbf{This is a clarity result, not new power.}  The apparatus does not extend
what VR can prove.  It contributes no axiom of its own to any transited object;
it provides a conservative spine and an honest, bounded library of
\texttt{Factorisable} bridges, and --- the point of this chapter --- it lets us
say exactly where the axiom cost of a transit lives, and exactly where the
witness method stops working.  The universal form of conservativity is a meta
statement about the kernel, exhibited on representatives, not proved as a single
internal theorem (Section~\ref{sec:transit_conservativity}).

\textbf{Two tracks, one of them out of reach.}  The apparatus has a predicate
track and a reference track (Finding~S3-A).  The four providers of this chapter
all live in the predicate track.  The reference track was examined and yielded no
clean witness method; structurally, non-descent there reduces to the need for
\texttt{Classical.choice}, which cannot be witnessed
(Finding~TR-R1, Section~\ref{sec:transit_findings}).  This is the sharpest result
of the chapter.

\section{Conservativity (I)}
\label{sec:transit_conservativity}

The apparatus is axiom-neutral: the internal carriers of conservativity are
existing \axiomprofile{[]} lemmas, re-used, not re-proved.

\begin{theorem}[Internal carriers of conservativity]
\label{thm:transit_carriers}
\lean{VR.Apparatus.operand_determines_operational,
      VR.Apparatus.factorisable_implies_isModeBOp,
      VR.Apparatus.IsModeBOp_of_factorisable,
      VR.Apparatus.Factorisable.lift_val,
      VR.Apparatus.IsModeBOp.compose}
\leanok
\uses{chap:apparatus}
The apparatus path from a witness to a Mode~B operation, the transparency of the
lift, and the composition of transits are all axiom-free.
\texttt{operand\_determines\_operational}, \texttt{factorisable\_implies\_isModeBOp},
\texttt{IsModeBOp\_of\_factorisable}: \axiomprofile{[]} (the apparatus reductions).
\texttt{Factorisable.lift\_val}: \axiomprofile{[]}, and it is \texttt{rfl} --- the
transited operation \emph{definitionally is} the operation on the operational
subtype (transparency).  \texttt{IsModeBOp.compose}: \axiomprofile{[]} --- composing
two transits injects no axiom.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{The universal form is meta.}  The statement ``for all $f$,
$\operatorname{axioms}(\operatorname{transit} f) = \operatorname{axioms}(f)$'' is a
kernel-level fact: \texttt{\#print axioms} is not an internal predicate one can
quantify over.  We therefore \emph{exhibit} conservativity on representatives
(Section~\ref{sec:transit_audit}), exactly as every chapter of the cycle already
does with its axiom-audit footers, and prove only the schematic internal carriers
above.  No single universal internal theorem is claimed.

\section{The four-source decomposition of cost}
\label{sec:transit_decomposition}

The central observation.  Across the representative transited objects audited in
Section~\ref{sec:transit_audit}, the axiom profile is exhibited as a decomposition
into four named sources, with the apparatus contributing to none:
\[
  \operatorname{axioms}(\text{transit})
  \;\;\text{is exhibited as}\;\;
  \underbrace{\text{operation}}_{\text{e.g.\ Riesz, subgroup image}}
  \;\oplus\;
  \underbrace{\text{pointwise witness}}_{[\,]}
  \;\oplus\;
  \underbrace{\text{aggregation}}_{\texttt{Finset.sum}}
  \;\oplus\;
  \underbrace{\text{carrier encoding}}_{\text{Finding TR-FW1}}
  \;\oplus\;
  \underbrace{\text{apparatus}}_{\varnothing}.
\]
The operation source is itself a spectrum: algebraic infrastructure contributes
\texttt{[propext]} (subgroup image), analytic operations contribute
\texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} (Riesz); both are operation-side,
neither is apparatus (Finding~TR-C1).  Aggregation over an explicit \texttt{Finset}
contributes the choice-free \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}.  Carrier encoding is an
\emph{artefact}: indexing by \texttt{Fintype}/\texttt{Finset.univ} inflates to the
full ceiling, and is removable (Finding~TR-FW1).

\section{The witness library (II)}
\label{sec:transit_library}

Leverage is a bounded library of Tier-2 domain-structure $\to$
\texttt{Factorisable} bridges, each buying one family of transits.  The bridges
fall into two kinds, distinguished by their achievable axiom profile:

\textbf{Pointwise bridges (target \axiomprofile{[]}).}  Evaluate the witness at a
single named operand; no summation.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-generator pointwise bridge]
\label{thm:transit_finiteGen}
\lean{VR.Transit.HasFiniteGeneratorStructure,
      VR.Transit.finiteGen_provides_factorisable}
\leanok
\uses{chap:apparatus}
\texttt{HasFiniteGeneratorStructure}\,$(T\,\iota)$ carries an axiom-free generator
map \texttt{gens : $\iota \to T$} (\axiomprofile{[]}).
\texttt{finiteGen\_provides\_factorisable}: an operation agreeing with a globally
operational $g$ on every generator is \texttt{Factorisable} at each generator
(\axiomprofile{[]}).  This mirrors \texttt{separability\_provides\_factorisable}
with a finite index in place of a dense sequence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Located structural bridge (realised Level~B)]
\label{thm:transit_located}
\lean{VR.Transit.located_witness_operational,
      VR.Transit.located_provides_factorisable}
\leanok
\uses{chap:audit, chap:apparatus}
For an operational normable functional $f$ on an operational located subspace
$M \le E$, the located structure \emph{supplies} the witness $g := f \circ P_M$
(orthogonal projection), not merely the evaluation points.
\texttt{located\_witness\_operational}: $g$ is globally operational, re-using
\texttt{fn\_computable\_everywhere}.  \texttt{located\_provides\_factorisable}: any
operation agreeing with $g$ on the dense sequence is \texttt{Factorisable} there.
Both \axiomprofile{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} --- faithful, source is
the \emph{operation} (projection via completeness), apparatus $\varnothing$.  This
delivers the evaluation-point-level (Level~B) \texttt{Factorisable} that
Chapter~\ref{chap:apparatus} (separability) left deferred; the witness $g = f\circ
P_M$ is structural, distinct from the self-witnessing Riesz bridge --- not a
re-skin.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{Aggregating bridge (target choice-free \axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]}).}
Combining a witness over a finite operand set routes through \texttt{Finset.sum};
the achievable target is choice-free, not \texttt{[]}.

\begin{theorem}[Additive-span aggregating bridge]
\label{thm:transit_finiteSpan}
\lean{VR.Transit.finiteSpan_provides_factorisable}
\leanok
\uses{thm:transit_finiteGen, chap:apparatus}
\texttt{finiteSpan\_provides\_factorisable}: for additive maps agreeing on the
generators, agreement propagates to every $\mathbb{Z}$-combination over an explicit
\texttt{Finset} of indices, so the operation is \texttt{Factorisable} at any such
combination (\axiomprofile{[propext, Quot.sound]}).  This is the
operand-not-operation principle (Finding~S4-A) at the linear-combination level: the
output's operationality is fixed by the operand's explicit coordinates, not by the
map.  \texttt{Classical.choice} is absent --- finite additive transit is constructive.
\end{theorem}

The library thus spans density (separability, Chapter~\ref{chap:apparatus}),
completeness/projection (located), and finiteness (finite generators, in both a
pointwise and an aggregating tier).

\section{Findings}
\label{sec:transit_findings}

\textbf{Finding~TR-R1 (headline): the witness method reaches exactly as far as the
obstacle is witnessable.}  The four providers live in the predicate track, where
the obstacle is concrete --- density, projection, coordinates --- and a witness can
be exhibited.  The reference track (setoids/quotients, $\texttt{ZFSet} =
\texttt{Quotient}\,\texttt{PSet.setoid}$) was examined and yielded no clean witness
method: a natural non-descending operation (selection) is not totally and purely
expressible (one cannot extract an element of an arbitrary index type
\texttt{x.Type}); the only total version uses \texttt{Classical.choice}, whose
selection is opaque, so the defining non-descent is not even provable.  Structurally,
then, the reference track's non-descending content coincides with the need for
\texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Classical.choice} is precisely what cannot be
witnessed.  The operationalisation-by-witness method
therefore works exactly where the obstruction is concrete and witnessable, and fails
exactly where the obstruction is choice.  This is a characterisation of the method's
reach, and it complements Finding~S3-A from the Mode~B side.  The \texttt{RefFactorisable}
abstraction is dropped (no nontrivial instance; recognition discipline).

\textbf{Finding~TR-FW1 (two faces, both removable; the A15 family).}  Finite
additive transit cost lives in the carrier \emph{encoding}, not the algebra.
Face~1: indexing by \texttt{Fin}/\texttt{Fintype} and summing over
\texttt{Finset.univ} inflates to \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} ---
even a \texttt{rfl} identity carries it --- whereas an explicit \texttt{Finset} keeps
the sum choice-free.  Face~2: a \texttt{Finset} field placed in the Tier-2 class
contaminates the class type with \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]} and the pointwise tier
inherits it; keeping the class to its axiom-free generator map restores \texttt{[]}.
Rule canonised: axiom-bearing data belongs to the aggregating bridge's arguments,
never to a Tier-2 class shared with pointwise bridges.

\textbf{Finding~TR-C1: the operation source is a spectrum.}
\texttt{image\_isOperationalAddSubgroup\_isModeBOp} is \texttt{[propext]} (algebraic
infrastructure), \texttt{riesz\_extension\_isModeBOp} is \texttt{[propext,
Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} (analytic); both attribute to the operation, neither
to the apparatus.

\section{Honest scope}
\label{sec:transit_scope}

\textbf{Predicate track only.}  The witness library and the exhibited
conservativity are predicate-track results (Finding~TR-R1).

\textbf{Both extension candidates were examined and dropped, with reasons.}  An
arithmetic/computability provider is redundant: \texttt{IsComputableReal} is
\emph{already defined} as carrying a rational algorithm with a convergence modulus,
so a ``modulus provider'' restates the existing predicate, and the corpus
deliberately does not use mathlib's \texttt{Computable} (no \texttt{Computable} proofs
for $\mathbb{Q}$-arithmetic); over $\mathbb{N}$ the output predicate is trivial and
the bridge collapses.  A reference-track provider does not exist (Finding~TR-R1).
The ceiling has been probed from both sides; the spanning set is four predicate-track
providers, not vapour.

\textbf{Universal conservativity is meta.}  Exhibited on representatives, not an
internal theorem (Section~\ref{sec:transit_conservativity}).

\section{Position relative to existing work}
\label{sec:transit_related}

The angle of VR-Transit --- axiom attribution and transit conservativity as a lens
--- is distinct from proof mining (extracting bounds from classical proofs), reverse
mathematics (calibrating theorems against subsystems), Bishop-style constructive
analysis (rebuilding analysis without excluded middle), and computable analysis
(representations and Type-2 effectivity).  Chapter~\ref{chap:apparatus} already
situates the cycle against these schools (its Remarks on related work); the delta
here is only that VR-Transit measures the \emph{apparatus's own} axiom contribution
(zero) and attributes each transit's cost to a named source, rather than re-founding
any mathematics.
